## Introduction

NASA's 2023 independent study concluded that the greatest obstacle to understanding unidentified anomalous phenomena (UAP) is not necessarily the rarity of reports but the poor quality and inconsistency of the information collected. Rather than treating UAP solely as a mystery, the study argued that they should be approached as a scientific and aviation-safety problem. That shift places the emphasis on better reporting: preserving accurate details while memories are fresh, capturing sensor information before it is lost, and encouraging more people to report unusual observations without fear of ridicule.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://science.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/uap-independent-study-team-final-report.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: science.nasa.gov">[NASA Science]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">science.nasa.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Science NASA</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UNIDENTIFIED ANOMALOUS PHENOMENA...</span></span></span>

For discussions surrounding UFO-related claims, including those connected with figures such as Gary McKinnon, this distinction matters. Extraordinary interpretations cannot be tested if the underlying observations lack reliable metadata or cannot be independently verified. Standardised reporting is therefore intended to improve the quality of evidence, regardless of whether a case ultimately has an ordinary explanation or remains unresolved.

## Could Better Reporting Solve More UFO Cases?

NASA's recommendations were not aimed at proving or disproving extraterrestrial explanations. Instead, they focused on increasing the proportion of cases that can be confidently explained by ensuring investigators receive consistent, complete and scientifically useful information.

When different witnesses describe the same event using different formats—or omit critical details altogether—investigators often cannot reconstruct what actually happened. Small omissions, such as an inaccurate time or missing weather conditions, may prevent comparison with aircraft tracks, satellite passes, astronomical objects or atmospheric phenomena.

A standard reporting framework helps preserve information that otherwise disappears within hours or days. It also makes reports easier to compare across multiple observers, locations and sensor systems. NASA identified systematic reporting as one of the foundations needed for any credible scientific study of UAP.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://science.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/uap-independent-study-team-final-report.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: science.nasa.gov">[NASA Science]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">science.nasa.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Science NASA</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UNIDENTIFIED ANOMALOUS PHENOMENA...</span></span></span>

## Which Details Reports Should Capture

NASA's report emphasised that useful investigations depend on far more than a photograph or eyewitness description. A standard report should preserve contextual information that allows independent analysis.

Important elements include:

* **Exact date and time**, preferably synchronised with standard time sources.

<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* **Precise location**, including altitude where relevant.
* **Direction of observation**, including compass bearing and elevation angle.
* **Weather and visibility**, including cloud cover, wind and atmospheric conditions.
* **Observer information**, such as whether the witness was a trained pilot, controller or member of the public.
* **Original images or video**, rather than compressed or edited copies.
* **Sensor metadata**, including camera model, focal length, exposure settings and calibration details.
* **Platform movement**, such as whether observations were made from a moving aircraft or vehicle.
* **Additional witnesses or independent sensors**, including radar, infrared systems or other recordings.

</div>

These details allow analysts to test ordinary explanations systematically rather than relying on subjective impressions. Even if a report remains unidentified, investigators can better understand what possibilities have already been ruled out.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://science.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/uap-independent-study-team-final-report.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: science.nasa.gov">[NASA Science]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">science.nasa.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Science NASA</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UNIDENTIFIED ANOMALOUS PHENOMENA...</span></span></span>

## How Stigma Reduces Useful Observations

NASA devoted unusual attention to the social problem of reporting. The study concluded that stigma discourages both civilian and professional observers from reporting unusual events, reducing the amount of usable information available for analysis.

Commercial pilots, military personnel and air traffic controllers may hesitate to report unfamiliar observations if they fear ridicule or concern about their professional reputation. Members of the public may likewise avoid reporting because they expect to be associated with conspiracy theories rather than routine aviation safety.

NASA argued that treating UAP as a legitimate scientific and operational reporting category—rather than as evidence for any particular explanation—could improve participation and increase the number of high-quality reports. This approach separates the act of reporting from assumptions about what was observed.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://science.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/uap-independent-study-team-final-report.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: science.nasa.gov">[nasa.gov]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">science.nasa.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Science NASA</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UNIDENTIFIED ANOMALOUS PHENOMENA...</span></span></span>

## Why Aviation Reporting Offers a Useful Model

One practical recommendation involved learning from existing aviation safety systems rather than creating an entirely new investigative culture.

Pilots already report mechanical failures, weather hazards and near misses through structured reporting systems. These systems work because they encourage detailed, standardised accounts that can be analysed consistently.

NASA's study suggested that similar practices could improve UAP reporting, particularly when observations involve trained aviation professionals. Standard forms reduce ambiguity, encourage complete descriptions and make it easier to compare reports across agencies and over time. NASA also highlighted opportunities to work with the Federal Aviation Administration and to expand reliable reporting from commercial pilots.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.nasa.gov/news-release/update-nasa-shares-uap-independent-study-report-names-director/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nasa.gov">[NASA]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nasa.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">UPDATE: NASA Shares UAP Independent Study Report; Names Director</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UPDATE: NASA Shares UAP Independent Study Report; Names Director - NASA...</span></span></span>

Independent aviation safety specialists have similarly argued that a single reporting pathway with clear guidance and standard templates would reduce fragmented data collection and improve traceability, although these proposals represent recommendations rather than official policy.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.narcap.org/faa-reporting-recommendation" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: narcap.org">[NARCAP]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">narcap.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Platte, John-Michael Guttierez, Ted Roe, Ryan Graves, 2023 — National Aviation Reporting Center on Anomalous PhenomenaJune 14, 2023...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: June 14, 2023</span></span></span>

## Better Data Also Improves Automated Analysis

Standardisation is important not only for human investigators but also for modern analytical tools.

NASA noted that artificial intelligence and machine-learning systems require well-characterised, consistently collected data. If reports vary widely in format or omit essential information, automated systems struggle to distinguish meaningful anomalies from ordinary objects, sensor errors or incomplete observations.

Consistent reporting makes it possible to:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--metric" markdown="1">

* compare similar incidents objectively;
* identify recurring patterns across large datasets;
* cross-reference reports with weather, astronomical and aircraft databases;
* detect reporting biases; and
* improve statistical confidence when evaluating rare events.

</div>

## Where Reporting Still Falls Short of Proof

Even a perfectly completed report does not establish the nature of an observed object.

Eyewitness testimony can remain incomplete. Cameras may lack sufficient resolution. Radar data may be unavailable. Independent measurements may be missing altogether. Standardisation improves evidence quality, but it cannot create information that was never recorded.

Similarly, an unresolved report should not automatically be interpreted as evidence of advanced technology or extraterrestrial origin. NASA repeatedly stressed that "unidentified" simply means the available information is insufficient for a confident explanation. Better reporting increases the likelihood that future cases can be resolved, but some observations may remain ambiguous because of unavoidable observational limitations.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://science.nasa.gov/uap/faqs/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: science.nasa.gov">[NASA Science]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">science.nasa.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Science UAP FAQs</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">NASA ScienceUAP FAQs - NASA ScienceMay 8, 2026...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: May 8, 2026</span></span></span>

## What This Means for Historical UFO Claims

The emphasis on reporting standards also provides a useful framework for assessing older UFO stories.

Historical cases often rely on recollections recorded years after the event, incomplete documentation, missing originals or uncertain chains of custody. Without consistent records containing timing, location, environmental conditions and sensor information, later investigators cannot easily distinguish between competing explanations.

That does not prove historical claims are false, but it does explain why many remain unresolved decades later. NASA's recommendations suggest that future investigations are more likely to produce reliable conclusions if observations are documented using consistent scientific standards from the outset rather than reconstructed long afterwards.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://science.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/uap-independent-study-team-final-report.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: science.nasa.gov">[NASA Science]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">science.nasa.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Science NASA</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UNIDENTIFIED ANOMALOUS PHENOMENA...</span></span></span>

    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-trigger-market-label]')).forEach(function (label) {
      label.textContent = selectedText;
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-domain-label]')).forEach(function (label) {
      label.textContent = domainLabel(marketId);
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-option]')).forEach(function (button) {
      button.setAttribute('aria-selected', button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-option') === marketId ? 'true' : 'false');
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-button]')).forEach(function (button) {
      var active = button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button') === marketId;
      button.setAttribute('aria-pressed', active ? 'true' : 'false');
    });
    section.setAttribute('data-ebay-selected-market', marketId);
    if (persist) {
      try { if (window.localStorage) localStorage.setItem('phoenix-ebay-market', marketId); } catch (err) {}
    }
    closeMenu(section);
  }
  sections.forEach(function (section) {
    if (section.getAttribute('data-ebay-localized-init') === '1') return;
    section.setAttribute('data-ebay-localized-init', '1');
    var available = availableMarkets(section);
    var marketId = defaultMarket;
    if (window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation && window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation.inferMarket) {
      marketId = window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation.inferMarket(available, {
        storageKey: 'phoenix-ebay-market',
        defaultMarket: defaultMarket
      });
    } else if (available.indexOf(defaultMarket)< 0) {
      marketId = available[0] || defaultMarket;
    }
    var select = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-select]');
    if (select) {
      select.addEventListener('change', function () {
        applyMarket(section, select.value, true);
      });
    }
    var trigger = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-trigger]');
    var menu = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-menu]');
    if (trigger && menu) {
      trigger.addEventListener('click', function (event) {
        event.stopPropagation();
        var expanded = trigger.getAttribute('aria-expanded') === 'true';
        menu.hidden = expanded;
        trigger.setAttribute('aria-expanded', expanded ? 'false' : 'true');
        var picker = trigger.closest('.fr-ebay-market-picker');
        if (picker) picker.classList.toggle('fr-ebay-market-picker--open', !expanded);
      });
      Array.prototype.slice.call(menu.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-option]')).forEach(function (button) {
        button.addEventListener('click', function (event) {
          event.stopPropagation();
          applyMarket(section, button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-option'), true);
        });
      });
      document.addEventListener('click', function () { closeMenu(section); });
    }
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-button]')).forEach(function (button) {
      button.addEventListener('click', function () {
        applyMarket(section, button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button'), true);
      });
    });
    applyMarket(section, marketId, false);
  });
})();
