## Introduction

Gary McKinnon and Mathew Bevan are often described together as British “UFO hackers”, but the comparison can be misleading. Both men later said that an interest in UFO secrecy influenced their [unauthorised access]({{ 'unauthorised-access/' | relative_url }}) to American military and government computer systems. The difference lies in how central that interest was to their activities and what they expected to find. McKinnon openly framed his hacking as a direct search for evidence of suppressed UFO technology, antigravity research and secret space programmes. By contrast, Bevan's UFO interest formed part of a broader fascination with military networks, advanced aircraft and alleged government cover-ups rather than a single-minded campaign to prove extraterrestrial claims. Contemporary evidence also treats Richard Pryce differently again: while he operated alongside Bevan during the [Rome Laboratory]({{ 'rome-laboratory/' | relative_url }}) intrusions, the historical record provides much weaker evidence that UFOs were his primary motivation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://spectrum.ieee.org/the-autistic-hacker" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: spectrum.ieee.org">[ieee.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">spectrum.ieee.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Spectrum Gary Mc Kinnon: The Autistic Hacker</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">IEEE SpectrumGary McKinnon: The Autistic Hacker - IEEE SpectrumJune 27, 2011...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: June 27, 2011</span></span></span>

## McKinnon's stated search for hidden technology

McKinnon consistently described his motives in unusually explicit terms. Rather than claiming that UFOs became an interest after his intrusions, he said they were the reason he targeted NASA and US military systems in the first place. Inspired by material from the [Disclosure Project]({{ 'disclosure-influence/' | relative_url }}) and longstanding beliefs that governments were concealing advanced propulsion and energy technologies, he sought documentary proof rather than financial gain or espionage.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://spectrum.ieee.org/the-autistic-hacker" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: spectrum.ieee.org">[IEEE Spectrum]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">spectrum.ieee.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Spectrum Gary Mc Kinnon: The Autistic Hacker</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">IEEE SpectrumGary McKinnon: The Autistic Hacker - IEEE SpectrumJune 27, 2011...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: June 27, 2011</span></span></span>

His own public explanations repeatedly focused on three related objectives:

* finding evidence that UFO encounters were being concealed;
* locating information about antigravity or "[free energy]({{ 'free-energy/' | relative_url }})" technology;
* confirming the existence of secret space operations beyond publicly acknowledged programmes.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.checktheevidence.com/wordpress/2006/05/06/old-news-1805/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: checktheevidence.com">[Check The Evidence]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">checktheevidence.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Check The Evidence Hacker – Gary Mc Kinnon – BBC Interview – Check The Evidence</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Check The Evidence Hacker – Gary Mc Kinnon – BBC Interview – Check The Evidence</span></span></span>

The claims for which McKinnon became famous all reflect those expectations. He later said he encountered references to "non-terrestrial officers", lists of unexplained ship transfers and a high-resolution image of what he believed was a non-human spacecraft. None of these claims has been independently verified, and no documentary evidence from the alleged files has ever been publicly produced. Nevertheless, these reported discoveries became central to McKinnon's public identity and explain why his case remains closely associated with UFO culture.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.theguardian.com/theguardian/2005/jul/09/weekend7.weekend2" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: theguardian.com">[theguardian.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">theguardian.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">The Guardian Game over &#124; Gary Mc Kinnon &#124; The Guardian</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Guardian Game over &#124; Gary Mc Kinnon &#124; The Guardian</span></span></span>

An important distinction is that McKinnon's motives remained remarkably consistent before, during and after the legal proceedings. Interviews over many years repeat essentially the same explanation: he believed hidden aerospace technologies existed and thought insecure [government networks]({{ 'network-blind-spots/' | relative_url }}) might contain proof.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.checktheevidence.com/wordpress/2006/05/06/old-news-1805/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: checktheevidence.com">[checktheevidence.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">checktheevidence.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Check The Evidence Hacker – Gary Mc Kinnon – BBC Interview – Check The Evidence</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Check The Evidence Hacker – Gary Mc Kinnon – BBC Interview – Check The Evidence</span></span></span>

## Bevan's interest in aircraft and alleged cover-ups

[Mathew Bevan]({{ 'mathew-bevan/' | relative_url }})'s own descriptions reveal a broader pattern of curiosity than McKinnon's. He has explained that he became interested in UFO conspiracies after discovering collections of documents on bulletin board systems discussing Roswell, alleged government cover-ups and rumours that hackers had disappeared while searching military networks. Those stories encouraged him to investigate military computers that might contain related material.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.kujimedia.com/confessions-of-a-hacker-by-mathew-bevan/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: kujimedia.com">[Kujimedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">kujimedia.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Kuji Media Corporation Ltd. » Blog Archive » Confessions of a hacker by Mathew BevanJune 26, 2008...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: June 26, 2008</span></span></span>

However, Bevan's accounts describe a wider investigative interest than simply proving extraterrestrial life. His searches extended to:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* military research facilities;
* NASA-related systems;
* advanced aircraft and aerospace projects;
* classified defence information that might relate to alleged government secrecy.

</div>

Rather than describing one specific document or discovery that justified his efforts, Bevan characterised his objective as searching for evidence of a broader conspiracy surrounding the UFO phenomenon.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.kujimedia.com/tales-of-digital-crime-from-the-shadows-of-cyberspace-chapter-six/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: kujimedia.com">[Kujimedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">kujimedia.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Kuji Media Corporation Ltd. » Blog Archive » Tales of Digital Crime from the Shadows of Cyberspace – Chapter Six...</span></span></span>

His own retrospective comments also place personal circumstances alongside ideological ones. Bevan has spoken about being bullied at school and finding escape and status within early hacker communities. He described UFO research as giving direction to his hacking rather than completely defining it. That combination of curiosity, social isolation and technical exploration contrasts with McKinnon's much more focused narrative centred on suppressed technology.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.kujimedia.com/tales-of-digital-crime-from-the-shadows-of-cyberspace-chapter-six/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: kujimedia.com">[Kujimedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">kujimedia.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Kuji Media Corporation Ltd. » Blog Archive » Tales of Digital Crime from the Shadows of Cyberspace – Chapter Six...</span></span></span>

Another practical difference is that Bevan's activities formed part of the wider 1994 Rome Laboratory intrusions. Investigators documented extensive movement through interconnected military networks using compromised systems as stepping stones. ## Why their motives are often conflated

The two cases are frequently merged because both involved British hackers, American military systems and references to UFO secrecy. Yet the evidence supports different characterisations.

McKinnon made extraordinary claims about what he believed he had actually found. Whether accepted or rejected, those alleged discoveries became inseparable from his public story. Bevan, in contrast, is remembered mainly because he said UFO conspiracies motivated his search, not because he claimed to have uncovered comparable evidence. Contemporary reporting about the Rome Laboratory incident concentrated on the cybersecurity implications rather than sensational discoveries inside the compromised systems.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.theguardian.com/theguardian/2005/jul/09/weekend7.weekend2" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: theguardian.com">[theguardian.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">theguardian.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">The Guardian Game over &#124; Gary Mc Kinnon &#124; The Guardian</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Guardian Game over &#124; Gary Mc Kinnon &#124; The Guardian</span></span></span>

The distinction matters because later retellings sometimes imply that both hackers reported the same kinds of secret files or reached the same conclusions. The documentary record does not support that equivalence. McKinnon's reputation rests largely on his post-hack claims about hidden space programmes, whereas Bevan's reputation rests on his role in a landmark military network intrusion whose stated UFO motivation remained comparatively broad and exploratory.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://spectrum.ieee.org/the-autistic-hacker" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: spectrum.ieee.org">[ieee.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">spectrum.ieee.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Spectrum Gary Mc Kinnon: The Autistic Hacker</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">IEEE SpectrumGary McKinnon: The Autistic Hacker - IEEE SpectrumJune 27, 2011...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: June 27, 2011</span></span></span>

## Why Pryce's UFO motive is less certain

[Richard Pryce]({{ 'richard-pryce/' | relative_url }}) ("Datastream Cowboy") is often grouped with Bevan because both participated in the Rome Laboratory intrusion campaign, but the evidence for a UFO-centred motive is considerably weaker.

Official investigations, court reporting and technical reconstructions overwhelmingly describe Pryce in terms of his unauthorised access to Air Force, NASA and defence contractor systems. They document his methods and the chain of intrusions but provide little evidence that he publicly presented his hacking as a search for extraterrestrial information.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.airandspaceforces.com/app/uploads/1998/01/0198_Jan1998.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: airandspaceforces.com">[Air &amp; Space Forces Magazine]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">airandspaceforces.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Air &amp; Space Forces MagazineCollins innovatio:is come to life in an wn1atched variety af navigation and communications products that keep him...</span></span></span>

Later narratives sometimes associate Pryce with UFO-related interests because he operated within the same hacker milieu as Bevan and reached some of the same military systems. Even so, the available evidence does not place UFO secrecy at the centre of Pryce's own stated motivations in the way it clearly does for McKinnon or, to a lesser extent, Bevan.
