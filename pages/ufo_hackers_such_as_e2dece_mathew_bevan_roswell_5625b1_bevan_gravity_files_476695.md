## Introduction

The most enduring part of [Mathew Bevan]({{ 'mathew-bevan/' | relative_url }})'s account of his Roswell-motivated hacking is not the intrusion itself, but his later claim that he briefly read technical documents describing an advanced gravity-based propulsion system. According to Bevan, the material appeared to concern an aircraft capable of extraordinary performance using an unconventional reactor and a method of reducing the effects of inertia. However, no authenticated copy of these files has ever been produced, and no independent investigator has verified their contents. As a result, the "gravity files" occupy an unusual place in UFO history: they are influential as a personal testimony, but weak as documentary evidence.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ufohackers.org/hackers/mathew-bevan/bevan-overview" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufohackers.org">[UFO Hackers]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufohackers.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">UFO Hackers Bevan Overview &#124; www.ufohackers.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UFO Hackers Bevan Overview &#124; www.ufohackers.org</span></span></span>

## Bevan's Account of the Material

Years after his arrest, Bevan described accessing a computer at [Wright-Patterson]({{ 'wright-patterson/' | relative_url }}) Air Force Base that, according to him, required no password. On one system he recalled reading current technical documents and future project proposals that gave him the impression he had found plans for an anti-gravity propulsion system.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ufohackers.org/hackers/mathew-bevan/bevan-overview" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufohackers.org">[UFO Hackers]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufohackers.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">UFO Hackers Bevan Overview &#124; www.ufohackers.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UFO Hackers Bevan Overview &#124; www.ufohackers.org</span></span></span>

His recollections consistently included several technical elements:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* **An anti-gravity engine** that he believed could propel an aircraft at roughly **Mach 12 to Mach 15**.
* **A reactor-based power source**, accompanied by numerous technical figures and numerical values that he admitted he did not understand.
* **A "super heavy element"** described as the reactor's primary fuel.
* **A propulsion mechanism** that supposedly disturbed molecules in front of the craft, reducing or eliminating the inertial forces that would normally subject occupants to extreme acceleration.
* **A very small aircraft** rather than a conventional jet, with some later retellings describing a split-level arrangement placing the reactor below the crew compartment.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ufohackers.org/hackers/mathew-bevan/bevan-overview" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufohackers.org">[ufohackers.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufohackers.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">UFO Hackers Bevan Overview &#124; www.ufohackers.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UFO Hackers Bevan Overview &#124; www.ufohackers.org</span></span></span>

### Why He Connected the Files to Roswell

The documents themselves, as Bevan later described them, reportedly did **not** explicitly state that they involved extraterrestrial technology or the Roswell incident. Instead, Bevan made that connection because he had deliberately targeted Wright-Patterson believing that, according to long-standing UFO folklore, the base housed research connected with recovered alien technology.

Consequently, the chain of reasoning ran as follows:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--step-flow" markdown="1">

1. Wright-Patterson was believed by UFO enthusiasts to possess Roswell-related material.
2. Bevan believed he had located highly unconventional propulsion research.
3. He therefore inferred that the documents might describe technology derived from a recovered craft.

</div>

This distinction is important because the alleged Roswell connection came from Bevan's interpretation rather than from surviving quotations showing the files themselves made such a claim.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.trulyadventure.us/the-hacker" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: trulyadventure.us">[Truly*Adventurous]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">trulyadventure.us</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Truly*Adventurous The Hacker</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Truly*Adventurous The Hacker</span></span></span>

## How the Story Changed in Later Retellings

As Bevan's account circulated through UFO media, the descriptions often became more specific than his earliest recollections.

Early interviews focused on his uncertainty. He said the documents merely gave him the **impression** that they described an anti-gravity engine, and he openly questioned whether the material might even have been deliberate misinformation. He remarked that he could not be sure whether what he had found was genuine or intended to deceive intruders.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ufohackers.org/hackers/mathew-bevan/bevan-overview" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufohackers.org">[UFO Hackers]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufohackers.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">UFO Hackers Bevan Overview &#124; www.ufohackers.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UFO Hackers Bevan Overview &#124; www.ufohackers.org</span></span></span>

Later retellings frequently presented stronger assertions, including descriptions of:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* a fully operational anti-gravity prototype;
* a craft layout with defined crew and reactor compartments;
* propulsion powered by an ultra-heavy element;
* technology directly linked with alien spacecraft.

</div>

Many of these later descriptions trace back to repeated interviews and secondary UFO publications rather than to newly discovered documentary evidence. ## What Cannot Be Tested Without the Original Files

The principal evidential problem is straightforward: the documents no longer exist for independent examination.

No authenticated copies have been released publicly, no screenshots have been verified, and no official investigation has confirmed that the files contained the technical concepts Bevan later described. ## Why the Gravity Files Remain Controversial

The significance of Bevan's account lies less in proving advanced propulsion than in illustrating the limits of anecdotal evidence. His recollections have remained remarkably consistent on broad themes—an unusual propulsion concept, a reactor, an extremely high-speed aircraft, and uncertainty over what he was reading. At the same time, every major technical claim depends entirely on memory rather than preserved documentation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ufohackers.org/hackers/mathew-bevan/bevan-overview" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufohackers.org">[UFO Hackers]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufohackers.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">UFO Hackers Bevan Overview &#124; www.ufohackers.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UFO Hackers Bevan Overview &#124; www.ufohackers.org</span></span></span>

For historians of UFO-related hacking, the gravity files therefore represent an evidential dead end rather than a verified discovery.
