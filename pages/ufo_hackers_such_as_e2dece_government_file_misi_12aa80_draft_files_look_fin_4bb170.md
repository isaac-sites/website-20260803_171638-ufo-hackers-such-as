## Introduction

Government documents can appear remarkably authoritative long before they represent an approved government position. Draft memoranda, proposed directives and working papers are commonly prepared using official templates, classification markings, distribution lists and the names of senior offices because they are designed to move through formal review processes. When such documents are later discovered in archives, released under freedom of information laws or discussed in connection with cases such as UFO-motivated hacking by Gary McKinnon, their polished appearance can be mistaken for proof that they record settled institutional conclusions. In reality, the appearance of authority and the authority of a document are different questions. Understanding how government drafts are created, reviewed and preserved helps explain why authentic files can still represent ideas under discussion rather than official policy.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.archives.gov/guidance/records-management-guidance" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: archives.gov">[National Archives]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">archives.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">National Archives Records Management Guidance &#124; National Archives</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National ArchivesRecords Management Guidance &#124; National ArchivesMarch 11, 2021...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: March 11, 2021</span></span></span>

* Official departmental letterheads and templates.
* Security classifications or handling markings.
* Distribution lists naming senior officials.
* Signature blocks prepared for future approval.
* Formal legal or policy language.

None of these features necessarily indicates that the proposal completed the approval process. Instead, they are often prerequisites for internal circulation. CIA correspondence guidance, for example, distinguishes between multiple stages of preparation—including draft, final and revision—even though each stage may carry similar official formatting and control markings.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cia.gov/readingroom/document/cia-rdp74-00005r000200060045-8" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cia.gov">[CIA]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cia.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">CORRESPONDENCE STYLE &amp; PROCEDURES &#124; CIA FOIA (foia.cia.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">CORRESPONDENCE STYLE &amp; PROCEDURES &#124; CIA FOIA (foia.cia.gov)...</span></span></span>

This distinction is particularly important when isolated pages circulate online without their surrounding file history. A single page bearing an agency seal or classification banner can look definitive even if later pages show requested changes, objections or withdrawal.

## What Approval History and Versioning Can Reveal

The most reliable way to judge a government's position is not by appearance alone but by the document's approval trail.

### Indicators that a document remained a draft

Several clues often indicate that a file was still moving through an internal process:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* References to comments or coordination from other offices.
* Language requesting approval or concurrence.
* Revision numbers or labels such as "Draft", "Revised Draft" or "Working Copy".
* Missing signatures from approving officials.
* Notes indicating future circulation or consultation.

</div>

Government recordkeeping guidance also distinguishes between drafts and official file copies. The final record normally includes evidence of approval, while agencies may retain drafts and related materials because they document how decisions evolved. Electronic recordkeeping guidance specifically recommends identifying draft status, associating relevant drafts with the final document where appropriate and preserving version history when those drafts themselves qualify as records.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.archives.gov/records-mgmt/policy/agency-recordkeeping-requirements.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: archives.gov">[National Archives]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">archives.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">National Archives A Management Guide &#124; National Archives</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National Archives A Management Guide &#124; National Archives</span></span></span>

### Why multiple versions matter

Large organisations rarely produce only one version of an important document. Successive drafts may contain substantial differences in wording, recommendations or conclusions. Early language can be softened, removed or reversed after legal review, technical assessment or senior management discussion.

Consequently, finding one dramatic sentence in an early draft does not establish that the same statement survived into the approved version.

## Why Isolated Draft Language Is Easy to Overread

The risk of misunderstanding increases when only fragments of a document become public.

Without access to surrounding material, readers may be unable to determine:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* Whether the text was written by the agency or received from another organisation.
* Whether comments from reviewers challenged or rejected the language.
* Whether later revisions removed the disputed passages.
* Whether the proposal was approved, amended or abandoned entirely.

</div>

Government archives routinely preserve working material because it documents institutional activity rather than only successful outcomes. Records management guidance therefore treats drafts as potentially valuable records without implying that every draft became official policy.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.archives.gov/guidance/records-management-guidance" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: archives.gov">[National Archives]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">archives.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">National Archives Records Management Guidance &#124; National Archives</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National ArchivesRecords Management Guidance &#124; National ArchivesMarch 11, 2021...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: March 11, 2021</span></span></span>

This distinction is especially relevant in discussions surrounding alleged discoveries on government computer systems. An authentic draft found on an official network demonstrates that someone prepared or stored that document within the organisation. It does not, by itself, demonstrate that the organisation adopted every statement it contains.

## Why This Matters When Assessing UFO-Related Claims

In debates surrounding UFO-related document discoveries—including claims associated with Gary McKinnon's reported searches of government systems—the formal appearance of a document is sometimes presented as evidence that its contents represent official government conclusions.

That inference skips an essential step. Analysts, archivists and records professionals instead look for corroborating evidence such as:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* The complete document rather than isolated extracts.
* Metadata showing authorship and revision history.
* Evidence of approval by authorised officials.
* References to superseding versions.
* Related correspondence explaining how the document was handled.

</div>

Without that wider documentary context, it is difficult to distinguish between an approved directive, a proposed policy, a discussion paper or an abandoned idea.

## The Practical Lesson

Authenticity and institutional endorsement are separate questions. A document can genuinely originate from a [government network]({{ 'network-blind-spots/' | relative_url }}) while still representing an unfinished proposal, a staff recommendation or an internal discussion rather than settled policy.

For that reason, official formatting, classification markings and senior names should be treated as evidence that a document belonged within a government workflow—not as proof that its contents became the government's final position. The most reliable interpretation comes from examining approval history, revision records and the document's place within the wider administrative process rather than judging its authority from appearance alone.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cia.gov/readingroom/document/cia-rdp74-00005r000200060045-8" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cia.gov">[cia.gov]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cia.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">CORRESPONDENCE STYLE &amp; PROCEDURES &#124; CIA FOIA (foia.cia.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">CORRESPONDENCE STYLE &amp; PROCEDURES &#124; CIA FOIA (foia.cia.gov)...</span></span></span>

    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-option]')).forEach(function (button) {
      button.setAttribute('aria-selected', button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-option') === marketId ? 'true' : 'false');
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-button]')).forEach(function (button) {
      var active = button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button') === marketId;
      button.setAttribute('aria-pressed', active ? 'true' : 'false');
    });
    section.setAttribute('data-ebay-selected-market', marketId);
    if (persist) {
      try { if (window.localStorage) localStorage.setItem('phoenix-ebay-market', marketId); } catch (err) {}
    }
    closeMenu(section);
  }
  sections.forEach(function (section) {
    if (section.getAttribute('data-ebay-localized-init') === '1') return;
    section.setAttribute('data-ebay-localized-init', '1');
    var available = availableMarkets(section);
    var marketId = defaultMarket;
    if (window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation && window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation.inferMarket) {
      marketId = window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation.inferMarket(available, {
        storageKey: 'phoenix-ebay-market',
        defaultMarket: defaultMarket
      });
    } else if (available.indexOf(defaultMarket)< 0) {
      marketId = available[0] || defaultMarket;
    }
    var select = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-select]');
    if (select) {
      select.addEventListener('change', function () {
        applyMarket(section, select.value, true);
      });
    }
    var trigger = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-trigger]');
    var menu = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-menu]');
    if (trigger && menu) {
      trigger.addEventListener('click', function (event) {
        event.stopPropagation();
        var expanded = trigger.getAttribute('aria-expanded') === 'true';
        menu.hidden = expanded;
        trigger.setAttribute('aria-expanded', expanded ? 'false' : 'true');
        var picker = trigger.closest('.fr-ebay-market-picker');
        if (picker) picker.classList.toggle('fr-ebay-market-picker--open', !expanded);
      });
      Array.prototype.slice.call(menu.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-option]')).forEach(function (button) {
        button.addEventListener('click', function (event) {
          event.stopPropagation();
          applyMarket(section, button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-option'), true);
        });
      });
      document.addEventListener('click', function () { closeMenu(section); });
    }
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-button]')).forEach(function (button) {
      button.addEventListener('click', function () {
        applyMarket(section, button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button'), true);
      });
    });
    applyMarket(section, marketId, false);
  });
})();
