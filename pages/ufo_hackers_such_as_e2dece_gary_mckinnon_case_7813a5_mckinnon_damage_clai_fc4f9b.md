## Was McKinnon a Browser or a Saboteur?

The US indictment and later court filings portrayed McKinnon as someone who deliberately interfered with government computer systems after obtaining administrator access. Prosecutors alleged that the damage extended far beyond unauthorised browsing and included actions that impaired military operations and required extensive restoration work.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://publications.parliament.uk/pa/ld200708/ldjudgmt/jd080730/mckinn-1.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: publications.parliament.uk">[UK Parliament]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">publications.parliament.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">UK Parliament House of Lords</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UK ParliamentHouse of Lords - Mckinnon V Government of The United States of America and AnotherJuly 30, 2008...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: July 30, 2008</span></span></span>

## The Alleged Outages and Deletions

The most serious allegation concerned the US Army's [Military District]({{ 'network-shutdown/' | relative_url }}) of Washington (MDW) network.

According to the allegations summarised by the House of Lords, McKinnon deleted critical operating system files from nine computers. Prosecutors claimed these deletions caused the entire MDW network—comprising more than 2,000 computers—to become unavailable for approximately 24 hours, significantly disrupting government functions. The alleged disruption became one of the central examples cited throughout the extradition litigation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://publications.parliament.uk/pa/ld200708/ldjudgmt/jd080730/mckinn-1.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: publications.parliament.uk">[UK Parliament]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">publications.parliament.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">UK Parliament House of Lords</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UK ParliamentHouse of Lords - Mckinnon V Government of The United States of America and AnotherJuly 30, 2008...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: July 30, 2008</span></span></span>

US authorities also alleged that McKinnon deleted approximately 2,455 user accounts on another Army computer responsible for controlling access to a military network. The claimed consequence was that affected systems repeatedly rebooted and became inoperable until administrators restored them.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://publications.parliament.uk/pa/ld200708/ldjudgmt/jd080730/mckinn-1.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: publications.parliament.uk">[UK Parliament]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">publications.parliament.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">UK Parliament House of Lords</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UK ParliamentHouse of Lords - Mckinnon V Government of The United States of America and AnotherJuly 30, 2008...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: July 30, 2008</span></span></span>

A separate allegation involved the Naval Weapons Station Earle in New Jersey. Prosecutors asserted that McKinnon deleted computer logs from systems used to monitor information about Navy ships, including their location, staffing and operational readiness. According to the allegations, removing those logs rendered the installation's network of more than 300 computers inoperable and left it vulnerable to further intrusion. US authorities emphasised that this occurred during the period immediately following the 11 September 2001 terrorist attacks, increasing the perceived seriousness of the alleged disruption.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://publications.parliament.uk/pa/ld200708/ldjudgmt/jd080730/mckinn-1.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: publications.parliament.uk">[UK Parliament]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">publications.parliament.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">UK Parliament House of Lords</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UK ParliamentHouse of Lords - Mckinnon V Government of The United States of America and AnotherJuly 30, 2008...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: July 30, 2008</span></span></span>

Prosecutors further alleged that McKinnon copied operating system files containing account names and encrypted passwords from multiple Army, Navy and NASA computers, including hundreds of passwords associated with Naval Weapons Station Earle. Although copying credentials was distinct from the alleged disruption, it formed part of the overall picture presented by the prosecution.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://publications.parliament.uk/pa/ld200708/ldjudgmt/jd080730/mckinn-1.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: publications.parliament.uk">[UK Parliament]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">publications.parliament.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">UK Parliament House of Lords</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UK ParliamentHouse of Lords - Mckinnon V Government of The United States of America and AnotherJuly 30, 2008...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: July 30, 2008</span></span></span>

## The Financial Damage Claimed by Prosecutors

Public reporting consistently referred to an estimated loss of roughly **US$700,000**, although the legal record provides important context for what that figure represented.

The House of Lords noted that, for sentencing purposes in a potential guilty plea, US prosecutors were prepared to place the provable monetary loss within a range of **US$400,000 to US$1 million**. Importantly, the calculation was said to consist primarily of labour costs: the hours government employees spent assessing compromised systems and restoring them to service, multiplied by their hourly rates. The figure did **not** include broader economic losses resulting from networks being unavailable, nor did it include additional sentencing enhancements that prosecutors believed might have been available if the case went to trial.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://publications.parliament.uk/pa/ld200708/ldjudgmt/jd080730/mckinn-1.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: publications.parliament.uk">[UK Parliament]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">publications.parliament.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">UK Parliament House of Lords</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UK ParliamentHouse of Lords - Mckinnon V Government of The United States of America and AnotherJuly 30, 2008...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: July 30, 2008</span></span></span>

This distinction is often overlooked. The published damage estimate was not presented simply as the cost of replacing destroyed equipment but largely as the expense of investigation, forensic examination, recovery and remediation following the intrusions.

## McKinnon's Alternative Explanation

McKinnon consistently rejected the characterisation that he had intentionally sabotaged military systems.

He admitted obtaining administrator access and acknowledged leaving messages critical of US security practices. He also accepted that his actions could have temporarily affected systems while he explored them. However, he maintained that his purpose was to search for evidence relating to UFOs, suppressed technology and what he believed were hidden government programmes—not to damage infrastructure or interfere with military operations.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.wired.com/2006/04/terrorist-or-ufo-truth-seeker/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: wired.com">[wired.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">wired.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">terrorist or ufo truth seeker</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">? &#124; WIREDApril 28, 2006...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: April 28, 2006</span></span></span>

## What the Legal Record Actually Proves

The public record clearly establishes several points.

McKinnon admitted [unauthorised access]({{ 'unauthorised-access/' | relative_url }}) to numerous US military and NASA computers. The US government formally alleged extensive operational disruption, deletion of files, network outages and substantial recovery costs. Those allegations were repeatedly accepted as the factual basis for extradition proceedings because extradition hearings are generally concerned with legal sufficiency rather than determining guilt through a full trial.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://publications.parliament.uk/pa/ld200708/ldjudgmt/jd080730/mckinn-1.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: publications.parliament.uk">[UK Parliament]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">publications.parliament.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">UK Parliament House of Lords</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UK ParliamentHouse of Lords - Mckinnon V Government of The United States of America and AnotherJuly 30, 2008...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: July 30, 2008</span></span></span>

What the legal record does **not** establish is that those allegations were proved beyond reasonable doubt at trial. McKinnon was never tried in the United States because the UK Home Secretary ultimately blocked his extradition on [human rights]({{ 'human-rights/' | relative_url }}) grounds related to his health. Consequently:

* No jury assessed competing expert evidence about the alleged damage.
* Defence lawyers never cross-examined the government witnesses responsible for the technical claims.
* No criminal court reached factual findings resolving whether the outages occurred exactly as alleged, whether McKinnon directly caused every claimed failure, or whether the financial losses accurately reflected his conduct.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.gov.uk/government/news/latest-on-gary-mckinnon-case" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: GOV.UK">[GOV.UK]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">GOV.UK</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Latest on Gary Mc Kinnon case</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Latest on Gary McKinnon case - GOV.UK...</span></span></span>

As a result, the central factual dispute remains unresolved. The prosecution's account is extensively documented in indictments and court summaries, while McKinnon's competing explanation appears in interviews and legal arguments. The absence of a completed trial means neither version received a final judicial determination on the underlying facts.
