## Introduction

Gary McKinnon's case became a landmark example of how extradition law can be shaped by compelling medical evidence rather than by the seriousness of the alleged offences alone. By October 2012, the decisive legal question was no longer whether the United States had a valid prosecution, but whether extraditing McKinnon would create a real and immediate risk that he would take his own life. After reviewing extensive psychiatric evidence, independent medical advice and legal analysis, Home Secretary Theresa May concluded that the risk was so high that extradition would be incompatible with McKinnon's rights under the European Convention on [Human Rights]({{ 'human-rights/' | relative_url }}). The decision did not excuse the alleged hacking or create a general exemption for people with autism. Instead, it reflected an evidence-based assessment that the foreseeable consequences of extradition, detention and prosecution abroad created an unacceptable risk of catastrophic harm.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.gov.uk/government/speeches/gary-mckinnon-extradition-case-home-secretarys-statement" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: GOV.UK">[GOV.UK]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">GOV.UK</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Gary Mc Kinnon extradition case: Home Secretary&#x27;s statement</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Gary McKinnon extradition case: Home Secretary&#x27;s statement - GOV.UKOctober 16, 2012...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: October 16, 2012</span></span></span>

## How the psychiatric evidence changed by 2012

The outcome of McKinnon's case turned on the quality and evolution of the medical evidence rather than on a sudden change in extradition law.

When the extradition proceedings began, his [mental health]({{ 'mental-health/' | relative_url }}) was not the central issue. McKinnon was diagnosed with Asperger's syndrome only in 2008, several years after the American extradition request. Subsequent assessments identified severe depressive illness alongside autism spectrum disorder and examined how those conditions interacted under extreme stress. Earlier appeals had therefore been decided before the full body of psychiatric evidence had been assembled.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://publications.parliament.uk/pa/ld200708/ldjudgmt/jd080730/mckinn-1.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: publications.parliament.uk">[UK Parliament]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">publications.parliament.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">UK Parliament House of Lords</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UK ParliamentHouse of Lords - Mckinnon V Government of The United States of America and AnotherJuly 30, 2008...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: July 30, 2008</span></span></span>

By 2012, the Home Secretary considered representations from multiple treating clinicians and also commissioned independent medical advice from experts recommended by the Chief Medical Officer. The review was therefore not confined to evidence submitted by the defence. After considering both independent psychiatric opinion and legal advice, she concluded that McKinnon's illness was genuine, severe and directly relevant to the human rights assessment required under the Extradition Act.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.gov.uk/government/speeches/gary-mckinnon-extradition-case-home-secretarys-statement" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: GOV.UK">[GOV.UK]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">GOV.UK</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Gary Mc Kinnon extradition case: Home Secretary&#x27;s statement</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Gary McKinnon extradition case: Home Secretary&#x27;s statement - GOV.UKOctober 16, 2012...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: October 16, 2012</span></span></span>

Crucially, the assessment was not based on a general concern that extradition is stressful. The medical evidence addressed McKinnon's particular vulnerability, including the predicted psychological impact of removal from his family, prolonged uncertainty, detention in a foreign country and the pressures of facing serious criminal proceedings overseas. The conclusion reached was that these combined factors would create an exceptionally high probability of suicide rather than merely increasing emotional distress.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.gov.uk/government/speeches/gary-mckinnon-extradition-case-home-secretarys-statement" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: GOV.UK">[GOV.UK]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">GOV.UK</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Gary Mc Kinnon extradition case: Home Secretary&#x27;s statement</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Gary McKinnon extradition case: Home Secretary&#x27;s statement - GOV.UKOctober 16, 2012...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: October 16, 2012</span></span></span>

## Why diagnosis alone was not enough

One of the most important lessons from the McKinnon case is that autism itself did not stop extradition.

The legal system distinguishes between having a medical diagnosis and demonstrating that extradition would produce consequences so severe that removal would violate fundamental rights. Many people with autism or depression are prosecuted or extradited without meeting that threshold.

In McKinnon's case, decision-makers focused on the relationship between his condition and the predicted consequences of extradition. Among the issues examined were:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--caution" markdown="1">

* the severity of his depressive illness alongside Asperger's syndrome;
* expert assessments of his suicidal intent and ability to act on it;
* the likelihood that separation from established family support would worsen his condition;
* whether American detention and healthcare arrangements could realistically reduce the identified risk;
* whether protective measures would merely supervise a determined individual rather than eliminate the danger.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.gov.uk/government/speeches/gary-mckinnon-extradition-case-home-secretarys-statement" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: GOV.UK">[GOV.UK]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">GOV.UK</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Gary Mc Kinnon extradition case: Home Secretary&#x27;s statement</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Gary McKinnon extradition case: Home Secretary&#x27;s statement - GOV.UKOctober 16, 2012...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: October 16, 2012</span></span></span>

</div>

This explains why earlier legal challenges failed. Previous courts accepted that extradition would be difficult and that McKinnon had mental health problems, but they were not persuaded that the available evidence crossed the exceptionally demanding legal threshold required to prevent extradition. As the psychiatric record became more comprehensive and expert opinion strengthened, the evidential picture changed.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://publications.parliament.uk/pa/ld200708/ldjudgmt/jd080730/mckinn-1.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: publications.parliament.uk">[UK Parliament]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">publications.parliament.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">UK Parliament House of Lords</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UK ParliamentHouse of Lords - Mckinnon V Government of The United States of America and AnotherJuly 30, 2008...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: July 30, 2008</span></span></span>

The decision therefore rested on individualised medical evidence rather than any assumption that autism automatically reduces criminal responsibility or bars international prosecution.

## How foreseeable harm shaped the human rights decision

The legal test applied in 2012 centred on foreseeability rather than sympathy.

Theresa May's statutory responsibility was limited to deciding whether extradition would breach McKinnon's human rights. She was not deciding whether he was guilty, whether the American prosecution was legitimate or whether the alleged hacking was serious. Those questions had already been addressed elsewhere in the legal process.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.gov.uk/government/speeches/gary-mckinnon-extradition-case-home-secretarys-statement" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: GOV.UK">[GOV.UK]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">GOV.UK</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Gary Mc Kinnon extradition case: Home Secretary&#x27;s statement</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Gary McKinnon extradition case: Home Secretary&#x27;s statement - GOV.UKOctober 16, 2012...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: October 16, 2012</span></span></span>

Instead, she asked whether surrendering McKinnon to the United States would predictably expose him to such a high risk of suicide that the United Kingdom would itself become responsible for violating his rights by authorising the extradition.

Her statement was carefully framed. She accepted both that McKinnon was accused of serious crimes and that he was seriously ill. The decisive finding was that the evidence established "such a high risk" of him ending his own life that extradition would be incompatible with his human rights. That wording reflected an assessment of foreseeable consequences rather than a humanitarian discretion exercised outside the law.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.gov.uk/government/speeches/gary-mckinnon-extradition-case-home-secretarys-statement" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: GOV.UK">[GOV.UK]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">GOV.UK</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Gary Mc Kinnon extradition case: Home Secretary&#x27;s statement</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Gary McKinnon extradition case: Home Secretary&#x27;s statement - GOV.UKOctober 16, 2012...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: October 16, 2012</span></span></span>

## Why the decision became a landmark

McKinnon's case established an influential principle for later extradition disputes involving severe mental illness.

It demonstrated that courts and ministers must examine the individual evidence rather than rely on diagnostic labels or the seriousness of the alleged offence alone. The case also reinforced that extradition decisions require a prospective assessment of foreseeable harm, particularly where credible psychiatric evidence indicates an exceptional risk of self-destruction.

The decision did not create a blanket rule against extraditing vulnerable defendants. Instead, it illustrated that where independent medical evidence demonstrates a real and immediate risk of catastrophic self-harm that cannot be adequately mitigated, the United Kingdom's human rights obligations may outweigh even a strong [public interest]({{ 'public-interest/' | relative_url }}) in extradition. That approach later informed judicial reasoning in other high-profile extradition cases involving severe mental illness, while remaining exceptional rather than routine.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.gov.uk/government/speeches/gary-mckinnon-extradition-case-home-secretarys-statement" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: GOV.UK">[gov.uk]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">GOV.UK</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Gary Mc Kinnon extradition case: Home Secretary&#x27;s statement</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Gary McKinnon extradition case: Home Secretary&#x27;s statement - GOV.UKOctober 16, 2012...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: October 16, 2012</span></span></span>
