## Introduction

Kona Blue is one of the clearest examples identified by the US Department of Defense's All-domain Anomaly Resolution Office (AARO) of how a proposed government programme later became confused with claims about a secret alien recovery operation. Rather than uncovering an active crash-retrieval project, AARO found that Kona Blue was a **proposed** Department of Homeland Security (DHS) Prospective Special Access Program (PSAP) that was never approved, never became operational, never received alleged extraterrestrial materials and never conducted reverse engineering.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.aaro.mil/Portals/136/PDFs/AARO_Historical_Record_Report_Vol_1_2024.pdf?emci=622cd777-b774-f011-8dc9-6045bda9d96b&amp;emdi=ea000000-0000-0000-0000-000000000001&amp;sourceid=1070813" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: aaro.mil">[AARO]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">aaro.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Unclassified Final DSD AARO Historical Report</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Unclassified Final DSD AARO Historical ReportMarch 8, 2024...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: March 8, 2024</span></span></span>

Within the broader debate over alleged hidden UFO programmes—including claims often linked to figures such as Gary McKinnon—Kona Blue is significant because it demonstrates how a proposal can later be remembered or described as though it had actually existed. According to AARO's investigation, the documentary record shows that the proposal failed before implementation, leaving no evidence that it evolved into the secret alien recovery programme described in later narratives.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.aaro.mil/Portals/136/PDFs/AARO_Historical_Record_Report_Vol_1_2024.pdf?emci=622cd777-b774-f011-8dc9-6045bda9d96b&amp;emdi=ea000000-0000-0000-0000-000000000001&amp;sourceid=1070813" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: aaro.mil">[AARO]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">aaro.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Unclassified Final DSD AARO Historical Report</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Unclassified Final DSD AARO Historical ReportMarch 8, 2024...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: March 8, 2024</span></span></span>

## What Kona Blue was designed to do

Kona Blue emerged after the cancellation of the Defence Intelligence Agency's Advanced Aerospace Weapon System Applications Program (AAWSAP). Supporters sought to establish a new [classified programme]({{ 'secret-projects/' | relative_url }}) under DHS that would continue research into unidentified anomalous phenomena (UAP) and create a secure framework for investigating reports involving alleged anomalous aerospace material.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.aaro.mil/Portals/136/PDFs/UAP_RECORDS_RESEARCH/History_and_Origin_of_KONA_BLUE_FINAL_508.pdf?ver=VWt5t7KtzTZzZ3bZoEAGqA%3D%3D" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: aaro.mil">[AARO]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">aaro.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-title">History and Origin of KONA BLUE</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">History and Origin of KONA BLUE...</span></span></span>

The proposal was unusually ambitious. According to the released planning documents, its intended activities included:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* restarting UAP investigations;
* collecting oral histories from former officials and contractors;
* organising governance for any anomalous materials that might eventually be obtained;
* providing a classified structure that advocates believed could oversee alleged legacy programmes if they existed.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.aaro.mil/Portals/136/PDFs/UAP_RECORDS_RESEARCH/History_and_Origin_of_KONA_BLUE_FINAL_508.pdf?ver=VWt5t7KtzTZzZ3bZoEAGqA%3D%3D" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: aaro.mil">[AARO]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">aaro.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-title">History and Origin of KONA BLUE</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">History and Origin of KONA BLUE...</span></span></span>

</div>

Crucially, these objectives were **prospective**. The proposal assumed that relevant materials or information might later become available. It did not describe an existing inventory of recovered spacecraft or biological remains under Kona Blue's control. AARO found no evidence that the programme ever acquired such material.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.aaro.mil/Portals/136/PDFs/UAP_RECORDS_RESEARCH/History_and_Origin_of_KONA_BLUE_FINAL_508.pdf?ver=VWt5t7KtzTZzZ3bZoEAGqA%3D%3D" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: aaro.mil">[AARO]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">aaro.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-title">History and Origin of KONA BLUE</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">History and Origin of KONA BLUE...</span></span></span>

## Why the proposal appeared more significant later

Part of the later confusion stemmed from the beliefs held by Kona Blue's advocates. AARO concluded that many supporters genuinely believed the US government already possessed hidden UAP technology and argued that Kona Blue should become the mechanism for transferring oversight of those alleged programmes into a properly managed Special Access framework.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Page%3AAARO_Historical_Record_Report_Volume_1_2024.pdf/35" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: en.wikisource.org">[Wikisource]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">en.wikisource.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Page:AARO Historical Record Report Volume</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Page:AARO Historical Record Report Volume 1 2024.pdf/35 - Wikisource, the free online library...</span></span></span>

This distinction is important. The proposal was based on the assumption that undisclosed recovery programmes already existed elsewhere. It was **not** itself evidence that such programmes had been located or verified.

According to AARO, proposal documents and interviews consistently showed that supporters expected Kona Blue to receive alleged recovered technology from other parts of government if those claims proved true. However, investigators found no documentation showing that any such transfer ever occurred.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Page%3AAARO_Historical_Record_Report_Volume_1_2024.pdf/35" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: en.wikisource.org">[Wikisource]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">en.wikisource.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Page:AARO Historical Record Report Volume</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Page:AARO Historical Record Report Volume 1 2024.pdf/35 - Wikisource, the free online library...</span></span></span>

The proposal therefore acquired a larger reputation than its actual implementation justified. Once the existence of planning documents became known years later, some observers interpreted the codename as proof that an operational crash-retrieval programme had existed. AARO's archival review reached the opposite conclusion: Kona Blue never progressed beyond the proposal stage.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.aaro.mil/Portals/136/PDFs/UAP_RECORDS_RESEARCH/History_and_Origin_of_KONA_BLUE_FINAL_508.pdf?ver=VWt5t7KtzTZzZ3bZoEAGqA%3D%3D" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: aaro.mil">[AARO]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">aaro.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-title">History and Origin of KONA BLUE</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">History and Origin of KONA BLUE...</span></span></span>

## Why officials rejected and closed the effort

The decisive reason Kona Blue never became an alien recovery programme is straightforward: DHS leadership declined to establish it.

According to AARO's historical review, the proposal initially received enough consideration for a Prospective Special Access Program request to be prepared. However, senior DHS officials ultimately rejected it because they concluded it lacked sufficient merit. The programme therefore never obtained formal approval.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Page%3AAARO_Historical_Record_Report_Volume_1_2024.pdf/35" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: en.wikisource.org">[Wikisource]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">en.wikisource.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Page:AARO Historical Record Report Volume</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Page:AARO Historical Record Report Volume 1 2024.pdf/35 - Wikisource, the free online library...</span></span></span>

That decision had several practical consequences:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* no operational Special Access Program was created;
* no dedicated funding or permanent organisation followed;
* no recovered craft or biological material was assigned to the programme;
* no reverse-engineering activities were undertaken under Kona Blue.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.aaro.mil/Portals/136/PDFs/UAP_RECORDS_RESEARCH/History_and_Origin_of_KONA_BLUE_FINAL_508.pdf?ver=VWt5t7KtzTZzZ3bZoEAGqA%3D%3D" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: aaro.mil">[AARO]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">aaro.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-title">History and Origin of KONA BLUE</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">History and Origin of KONA BLUE...</span></span></span>

## What AARO found after reviewing the records

When AARO investigated Kona Blue, it encountered interviewees who described it as though it had been a compartment protecting the retrieval and exploitation of "non-human biologics". Investigators compared those accounts against government records and reached a different conclusion.

The office determined that Kona Blue had been a proposed programme rather than an established one. It found no evidence that the programme ever received funding, materials, recovered spacecraft or biological specimens. Beyond the proposal package itself, AARO reported that no operational records existed because the programme had never been activated.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.aaro.mil/Portals/136/PDFs/UAP_RECORDS_RESEARCH/History_and_Origin_of_KONA_BLUE_FINAL_508.pdf?ver=VWt5t7KtzTZzZ3bZoEAGqA%3D%3D" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: aaro.mil">[AARO]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">aaro.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-title">History and Origin of KONA BLUE</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">History and Origin of KONA BLUE...</span></span></span>

This finding became one of the strongest documentary examples in AARO's broader conclusion that allegations of long-running government reverse-engineering programmes were not supported by verifiable records. The office argued that repeated claims often circulated within overlapping groups of individuals who sincerely believed such programmes existed, but whose accounts ultimately lacked independent corroborating evidence.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.aaro.mil/Portals/136/PDFs/AARO_Historical_Record_Report_Vol_1_2024.pdf?emci=622cd777-b774-f011-8dc9-6045bda9d96b&amp;emdi=ea000000-0000-0000-0000-000000000001&amp;sourceid=1070813" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: aaro.mil">[AARO]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">aaro.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Unclassified Final DSD AARO Historical Report</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Unclassified Final DSD AARO Historical ReportMarch 8, 2024...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: March 8, 2024</span></span></span>

## Why Kona Blue matters in the wider debate

Kona Blue remains noteworthy precisely because it was real as a **proposal** but not as an operational alien recovery programme.

Its existence demonstrates that some officials and contractors attempted to establish a classified framework capable of handling alleged anomalous materials if they could be obtained. It does **not** demonstrate that such materials were actually in government possession or that reverse engineering was underway.

For readers assessing claims about hidden UFO programmes—including those often associated with stories of secret government databases or concealed recovery efforts—Kona Blue illustrates the importance of distinguishing between planning documents, institutional aspirations and operational reality. According to the documentary record released by AARO, the proposal never crossed that line. DHS rejected it, it never became an authorised Special Access Program, and investigators found no evidence that it ever possessed or studied extraterrestrial technology.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.aaro.mil/Portals/136/PDFs/UAP_RECORDS_RESEARCH/History_and_Origin_of_KONA_BLUE_FINAL_508.pdf?ver=VWt5t7KtzTZzZ3bZoEAGqA%3D%3D" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: aaro.mil">[AARO]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">aaro.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-title">History and Origin of KONA BLUE</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">History and Origin of KONA BLUE...</span></span></span>
