## Introduction

The significance of RemotelyAnywhere lies in how it transformed a one-time compromise into persistent access. Instead of repeatedly searching for vulnerable machines or reusing the original authentication method, McKinnon could reconnect directly through software already running on the target computer. The choice of a legitimate administration product also illustrates how ordinary IT tools can become security liabilities when installed without authorisation.

## The Legitimate Tool That Became a Back Door

RemotelyAnywhere was not malicious software. Developed as a commercial remote-management product, it enabled authorised administrators to control Windows computers from another internet-connected machine through a web browser. Once installed, it provided capabilities comparable to sitting in front of the physical computer, including desktop access, file management and system administration.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.justice.gov/archive/usao/nj/Press/files/pdffiles/Older/edva_mckinnon_indictment.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: justice.gov">[Department of Justice]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">justice.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">edva mckinnon indictment</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Department of JusticeIN THE UNITED STATES DISTRICT COURT FOR THEMarch 26, 2026...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: March 26, 2026</span></span></span>

## Why Commercial Software Could Attract Less Suspicion

The allegations against McKinnon consistently distinguish between gaining access and maintaining it. After administrative privileges had been obtained, investigators alleged that he installed commercially available remote-administration software rather than relying solely on hacking utilities.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.justice.gov/archive/criminal/cybercrime/press-releases/2002/mckinnonIndict.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: justice.gov">[Department of Justice]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">justice.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Department of JusticeLondon, England Hacker Indicted Under Computer Fraud and Abuse Act For Accessing Military Computers (November 12, 20...</span></span></span>

This distinction mattered because commercial administration tools differed from many traditional "backdoor" programs of the period:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--caution" markdown="1">

* they were widely used by legitimate organisations;
* they offered stable, feature-rich remote administration;
* they often resembled ordinary system-management software rather than malware;
* security products of the early 2000s were generally less likely to treat authorised administration utilities as inherently suspicious.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.wired.com/2002/11/dot-mil-hackers-download-mistake/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: wired.com">[WIRED]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">wired.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">dot mil hackers download mistake</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Dot-Mil Hacker&#x27;s Download Mistake &#124; WIREDNovember 15, 2002...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: November 15, 2002</span></span></span>

Importantly, this did not make RemotelyAnywhere itself malicious. The software's intended purpose was lawful remote administration. The alleged abuse arose from installing it without permission on computers that had already been compromised.

## How Persistent Access Changed the Investigation

US prosecutors alleged that RemotelyAnywhere became a central component of maintaining continued access to several networks.

One indictment involving Naval Weapons Station Earle alleged that McKinnon installed RemotelyAnywhere on a port services computer and other connected systems, then used the previously installed software on multiple later occasions to reconnect over the internet. Prosecutors further alleged that, after obtaining password information, he later re-entered the network through the same installed software rather than recreating the original compromise.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.justice.gov/archive/criminal/cybercrime/press-releases/2002/mckinnonIndict2.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: justice.gov">[Department of Justice]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">justice.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Department of JusticeBritish National Charged with Hacking Into N.J. The broader Virginia indictment similarly alleges that after obtaining administrator privileges on military, NASA and other systems, McKinnon installed RemotelyAnywhere alongside other tools, copied password files and used compromised systems to discover additional vulnerable computers. In that account, the remote-administration software became part of a wider pattern of persistence rather than an isolated action.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.justice.gov/archive/usao/nj/Press/files/pdffiles/Older/edva_mckinnon_indictment.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: justice.gov">[Department of Justice]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">justice.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">edva mckinnon indictment</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Department of JusticeIN THE UNITED STATES DISTRICT COURT FOR THEMarch 26, 2026...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: March 26, 2026</span></span></span>

Within the prosecution's narrative, this transformed individual compromised computers into convenient return points, reducing the need to repeat the original authentication process every time access was required.

## The Download Records That Helped Trace McKinnon

Ironically, the use of legitimate commercial software also generated evidence that investigators could later exploit.

Contemporary reporting by *WIRED* described how investigators examined download logs maintained by the software distributor. According to the report, McKinnon downloaded a trial version of RemotelyAnywhere from the vendor's server using an internet connection associated with his UK internet service provider. The download process also allegedly included an email address supplied to obtain an evaluation code.
