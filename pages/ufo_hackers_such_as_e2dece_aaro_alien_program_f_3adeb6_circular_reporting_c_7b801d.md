## Introduction

One of the central conclusions of the US All-domain Anomaly Resolution Office (AARO) was not simply that it found no verifiable evidence of secret alien recovery programmes, but that many of the strongest-looking claims gained credibility through **circular reporting** rather than independent corroboration. In other words, stories that appeared to come from numerous witnesses often traced back to the same small network of people repeating or reinforcing one another's accounts.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.defense.gov/News/Transcripts/Transcript/Article/3702219/media-engagement-with-acting-aaro-director-tim-phillips-on-the-historical-recor/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: defense.gov">[U.S. Department of War]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">defense.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on defense.gov.</span></span></span>(https://www.defense.gov/News/Transcripts/Transcript/Article/3702219/media-engagement-with-acting-aaro-director-tim-phillips-on-the-historical-recor/)

This finding is particularly relevant when assessing claims linked to broader narratives about hidden government UFO programmes, including those that have shaped public discussion surrounding figures such as Gary McKinnon. If several apparently separate testimonies ultimately derive from a common source instead of separate firsthand evidence, the apparent weight of the claim can become misleading.

## What circular reporting means in UAP investigations

Circular reporting occurs when information passes repeatedly through a community until later versions are mistaken for fresh or independent evidence.

The process is not necessarily fraudulent. A person may honestly recount a story heard from someone they regard as trustworthy. Another listener then repeats that account elsewhere, perhaps omitting that it was second- or third-hand. After enough repetitions, the same underlying allegation appears to have multiple independent witnesses when it actually has only one unverified origin.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.defense.gov/News/Transcripts/Transcript/Article/3702219/media-engagement-with-acting-aaro-director-tim-phillips-on-the-historical-recor/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: defense.gov">[U.S. Department of War]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">defense.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on defense.gov.</span></span></span>(https://www.defense.gov/News/Transcripts/Transcript/Article/3702219/media-engagement-with-acting-aaro-director-tim-phillips-on-the-historical-recor/)

AARO argued that this mechanism substantially affected allegations of hidden crash-retrieval and reverse-engineering programmes. According to the office, many interviewees sincerely believed the stories they repeated, but belief itself did not transform repeated claims into empirical evidence.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.defense.gov/News/Transcripts/Transcript/Article/3702219/media-engagement-with-acting-aaro-director-tim-phillips-on-the-historical-recor/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: defense.gov">[U.S. Department of War]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">defense.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on defense.gov.</span></span></span>(https://www.defense.gov/News/Transcripts/Transcript/Article/3702219/media-engagement-with-acting-aaro-director-tim-phillips-on-the-historical-recor/)

## How witness networks created apparent corroboration

AARO reported that many modern allegations about concealed extraterrestrial technology originated within a relatively small circle of individuals connected through previous UAP-related activities, including participants associated with the former Advanced Aerospace Weapon System Applications Program (AAWSAP), later public advocacy efforts, and private paranormal research organisations. Rather than discovering numerous unrelated witnesses describing the same hidden programme, investigators frequently found interconnected relationships between sources.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Page%3AAARO_Historical_Record_Report_Volume_1_2024.pdf/36" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: en.wikisource.org">[Wikisource]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">en.wikisource.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Page:AARO Historical Record Report Volume</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Page:AARO Historical Record Report Volume 1 2024.pdf/36 - Wikisource, the free online library...</span></span></span>

This creates a common investigative problem:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* **Person A** hears an allegation from Person B.
* **Person C** independently hears the same allegation from Person A.
* Years later, investigators interview both A and C.
* Without reconstructing the chain of communication, their matching stories can appear to represent two independent confirmations.

</div>

In reality, both accounts stem from the same original unverified claim.

The same effect can occur through books, documentaries, podcasts, conferences, online forums and interviews. Once a narrative becomes well known, later witnesses may unintentionally absorb details from public discussion and later recall them as information obtained from private conversations. Memory research has long shown that repeated exposure can increase confidence in information without increasing its factual accuracy.

## Why repetition is not independent evidence

Independent corroboration requires more than agreement.

Investigators generally look for sources that developed their information separately, without influencing one another. If multiple witnesses rely—directly or indirectly—on the same original story, the evidential value is much lower than if each witness independently observed or documented the same event.

AARO's concern was therefore methodological rather than purely numerical. Ten interviews are not automatically stronger than one if all ten ultimately rely on a single unverified narrative.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.defense.gov/News/Transcripts/Transcript/Article/3702219/media-engagement-with-acting-aaro-director-tim-phillips-on-the-historical-recor/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: defense.gov">[U.S. Department of War]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">defense.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on defense.gov.</span></span></span>(https://www.defense.gov/News/Transcripts/Transcript/Article/3702219/media-engagement-with-acting-aaro-director-tim-phillips-on-the-historical-recor/)

The office stated that many interviewees could not provide:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* original documents;
* physical evidence;
* programme records;
* independently verifiable dates or locations; or
* firsthand technical observations that investigators could confirm.

</div>

Instead, allegations frequently rested on what others had previously claimed.

## A concrete example of the mechanism

The historical report describes how investigators mapped relationships among witnesses rather than treating each interview in isolation. According to AARO, numerous individuals making allegations about concealed alien technology had worked together, referred one another to investigators, appeared together in public discussions, or participated in overlapping UAP initiatives over many years.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Page%3AAARO_Historical_Record_Report_Volume_1_2024.pdf/36" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: en.wikisource.org">[Wikisource]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">en.wikisource.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Page:AARO Historical Record Report Volume</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Page:AARO Historical Record Report Volume 1 2024.pdf/36 - Wikisource, the free online library...</span></span></span>

This does not mean every witness deliberately coordinated testimony. Instead, AARO argued that long-standing professional and social connections allowed stories to circulate repeatedly within the same community.

The result was an information network where repeated accounts could gradually acquire an appearance of independent confirmation despite lacking new underlying evidence.

## Ways to distinguish repetition from independent evidence

The problem identified by AARO is not unique to UFO investigations. Historians, intelligence analysts and criminal investigators routinely separate independent corroboration from repeated hearsay.

Useful questions include:

* **What is the earliest identifiable source?** Can the claim be traced to a specific firsthand witness?
* **Did witnesses communicate beforehand?** Shared networks reduce independence.
* **Is there documentary support?** Contemporary records generally carry more evidential weight than recollections decades later.
* **Can physical evidence be examined?** Hardware, photographs with provenance, technical records and verified [chain-of-custody]({{ 'chain-of-custody/' | relative_url }}) documentation are more reliable than repeated oral testimony.
* **Do separate evidence streams converge?** Strong cases combine independent documents, physical evidence and unrelated witnesses rather than multiple retellings of the same story.

These principles help explain why investigators often focus less on the number of people making a claim than on whether their evidence originated independently.

A large body of consistent testimony may appear persuasive at first glance. However, if those accounts derive from a tightly connected network sharing the same underlying stories, their combined evidential strength is substantially weaker than the raw number of witnesses suggests.
