## Introduction

Gary McKinnon's alleged hacking campaign was not confined to a single military installation. According to United States indictments and later UK court records, prosecutors treated the intrusions between 2001 and 2002 as one connected campaign that reached across multiple branches of the US armed forces, the Pentagon and NASA. Rather than alleging a single spectacular breach, the case described repeated [unauthorised access]({{ 'unauthorised-access/' | relative_url }}) to vulnerable Windows systems that allowed movement from one compromised network to another. This broader pattern explains why the case became notable not only for McKinnon's claimed interest in UFO-related material, but also for the geographical and organisational spread of the affected [government networks]({{ 'network-blind-spots/' | relative_url }}).<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.justice.gov/archive/criminal/cybercrime/press-releases/2002/mckinnonIndict.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: justice.gov">[Department of Justice]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">justice.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Department of JusticeLondon, England Hacker Indicted Under Computer Fraud and Abuse Act For Accessing Military Computers (November 12, 20...</span></span></span>

## NASA and Pentagon Systems in the Record

NASA occupied a significant place in the prosecution's allegations because McKinnon was accused of accessing **16 NASA computers**. The indictments, however, primarily focused on the alleged unauthorised access, installation of remote-control software, copying of files and passwords, and resulting system damage rather than on the content of files he viewed. Official charging documents did not endorse or verify McKinnon's later public claims about discovering evidence relating to UFOs or secret space programmes.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.justice.gov/archive/criminal/cybercrime/press-releases/2002/mckinnonIndict.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: justice.gov">[Department of Justice]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">justice.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Department of JusticeLondon, England Hacker Indicted Under Computer Fraud and Abuse Act For Accessing Military Computers (November 12, 20...</span></span></span>

The Department of Justice also alleged that the campaign reached computers at the **Pentagon**, including two Pentagon systems identified in the Virginia indictment. ## How Separate Intrusions Formed One Campaign

Rather than portraying each incident as independent, investigators argued that McKinnon used compromised computers as stepping stones into additional government networks.

According to the House of Lords judgment, prosecutors alleged that he:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* identified internet-connected Microsoft Windows systems;
* extracted administrator account details and passwords;
* installed the commercial remote-access program **RemotelyAnywhere**;
* added further software to assist additional compromises and conceal activity; and
* used already-compromised computers to scan for further vulnerable government systems.

</div>

The judgment states that this approach enabled scans of more than **73,000 US government computers** in search of additional vulnerable hosts. The campaign's scale therefore came less from a single sophisticated exploit than from repeatedly finding similar weaknesses across multiple organisations.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://publications.parliament.uk/pa/ld200708/ldjudgmt/jd080730/mckinn-1.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: publications.parliament.uk">[UK Parliament]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">publications.parliament.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">UK Parliament House of Lords</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UK ParliamentHouse of Lords - Mckinnon V Government of The United States of America and Another...</span></span></span>

The Department of Justice similarly alleged that, once inside one network, McKinnon used that position to locate additional military and NASA systems, creating a chain of intrusions spanning multiple jurisdictions.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.justice.gov/archive/criminal/cybercrime/press-releases/2002/mckinnonIndict.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: justice.gov">[Department of Justice]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">justice.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Department of JusticeLondon, England Hacker Indicted Under Computer Fraud and Abuse Act For Accessing Military Computers (November 12, 20...</span></span></span>

## Geographic Reach Across the United States

The alleged campaign extended beyond one military base or state. [Court records]({{ 'court-record/' | relative_url }}) and indictments identify systems located in several parts of the United States, including:

* Army computers in **Virginia** and **Washington, DC**, supporting the [Military District]({{ 'network-shutdown/' | relative_url }}) of Washington.

## Why Prosecutors Viewed It as a Single Operation
