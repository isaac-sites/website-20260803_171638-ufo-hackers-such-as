## Introduction

The confirmed evidence about Gary McKinnon's access to NASA is both more specific and more limited than many later retellings suggest. The strongest [official record]({{ 'official-record/' | relative_url }}) is that investigators determined 15 NASA computers at five NASA centres had been compromised. What has never been publicly identified are the individual machines, the projects they supported, the files they contained or any mission-specific data allegedly viewed. That distinction is important because the fact that multiple centres were affected demonstrates that the intrusion reached geographically distributed NASA networks, but it does not support claims that McKinnon penetrated a single central repository containing all of NASA's most sensitive information.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.justice.gov/archive/criminal/cybercrime/press-releases/2002/mckinnonIndict.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: justice.gov">[Department of Justice]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">justice.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Department of JusticeLondon, England Hacker Indicted Under Computer Fraud and Abuse Act For Accessing Military Computers (November 12, 20...</span></span></span>

## Which NASA Centres Did McKinnon Actually Reach?

### What the Inspector General Confirmed

NASA's Inspector General informed Congress that investigators linked McKinnon to compromises involving 15 computers spread across five NASA centres. The testimony established the scale of the intrusion from an investigative perspective but deliberately avoided identifying the affected systems or describing the operational roles of those computers.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://oig.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/2024/02/final-written-statement-for-it-hearing-february-26-edit-v2.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: oig.nasa.gov">[NASA Office of Inspector General]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">oig.nasa.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">NASA Office of Inspector GeneralTestimony before the Subcommittee on Investigations and Oversight...</span></span></span>

This omission is significant. Public discussions often imply that investigators concealed dramatic discoveries, yet official testimony instead reflects a common practice in cybersecurity reporting: agencies frequently avoid naming compromised hosts, network architectures or sensitive systems because doing so could expose operational details or assist future attackers. The testimony therefore confirms the breadth of the compromise without revealing its technical targets.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://oig.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/2024/02/final-written-statement-for-it-hearing-february-26-edit-v2.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: oig.nasa.gov">[NASA Office of Inspector General]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">oig.nasa.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">NASA Office of Inspector GeneralTestimony before the Subcommittee on Investigations and Oversight...</span></span></span>

## What the Public Record Leaves Unnamed

The public record does **not** identify:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* which five NASA centres were affected;
* which individual computers were compromised;
* whether the machines were research workstations, administrative systems or infrastructure servers;
* which missions or scientific programmes, if any, those systems supported;
* what files McKinnon actually opened on each machine.

</div>

Because these details remain undisclosed, later claims that he accessed specific [classified projects]({{ 'misidentified-projects/' | relative_url }}), mission archives or comprehensive UFO repositories cannot be verified from official documentation. The available evidence simply does not establish that connection.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://oig.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/2024/02/final-written-statement-for-it-hearing-february-26-edit-v2.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: oig.nasa.gov">[NASA Office of Inspector General]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">oig.nasa.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">NASA Office of Inspector GeneralTestimony before the Subcommittee on Investigations and Oversight...</span></span></span>

This gap also explains why many narratives rely heavily on McKinnon's own recollections. His statements about searching [NASA image]({{ 'nasa-image/' | relative_url }}) directories or seeing particular files have been widely repeated, but they remain personal claims rather than findings corroborated through released forensic records or authenticated copies of the alleged material. Official investigators confirmed the [unauthorised access]({{ 'unauthorised-access/' | relative_url }}) itself, not the contents of every directory he later described.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.justice.gov/archive/criminal/cybercrime/press-releases/2002/mckinnonIndict.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: justice.gov">[Department of Justice]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">justice.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Department of JusticeLondon, England Hacker Indicted Under Computer Fraud and Abuse Act For Accessing Military Computers (November 12, 20...</span></span></span>

## Why Distributed Networks Limit Broad Claims

NASA has never operated as a single monolithic computer system. Its centres perform different functions, including human spaceflight, scientific research, aeronautics, engineering, mission operations and administration. Information is therefore distributed across many local networks and programme-specific systems rather than stored in one universal database.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.nasa.gov/organization/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nasa.gov">[NASA]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nasa.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">NASA Organization - NASAJuly 23, 2026...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: July 23, 2026</span></span></span>

That organisational structure limits what can reasonably be inferred from the confirmed compromise of five centres. Access to computers at several locations demonstrates lateral reach across multiple parts of NASA's infrastructure, but it does not imply unrestricted visibility into every programme or archive maintained by the agency. Even successful access to one host within a centre would not automatically grant access to unrelated projects, mission data or classified information elsewhere.

Similarly, the fact that NASA manages records on a centre-by-centre basis—as reflected even in its modern Freedom of Information procedures—illustrates how information is decentralised institutionally as well as technically. Records are maintained by individual centres and programmes rather than through a single agency-wide repository, reinforcing why broad conclusions about what McKinnon could have viewed exceed the available evidence.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.nasa.gov/foia/foia-contacts/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nasa.gov">[NASA]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nasa.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">FOIA Contacts</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">FOIA Contacts - NASA...</span></span></span>

## What the Five-Centre Evidence Actually Demonstrates

Taken on its own, the confirmed evidence supports three careful conclusions.

First, investigators established that the intrusion extended beyond a single isolated NASA location, making it a distributed compromise rather than an accidental encounter with one vulnerable computer.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://oig.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/2024/02/final-written-statement-for-it-hearing-february-26-edit-v2.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: oig.nasa.gov">[NASA Office of Inspector General]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">oig.nasa.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">NASA Office of Inspector GeneralTestimony before the Subcommittee on Investigations and Oversight...</span></span></span>

Second, the official record intentionally withholds enough technical detail that no reliable reconstruction of the affected systems is possible from public documents alone. There is no authoritative list of the compromised machines, their functions or the files they contained.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://oig.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/2024/02/final-written-statement-for-it-hearing-february-26-edit-v2.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: oig.nasa.gov">[NASA Office of Inspector General]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">oig.nasa.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">NASA Office of Inspector GeneralTestimony before the Subcommittee on Investigations and Oversight...</span></span></span>

Third, because the affected systems remain unidentified, the evidence neither confirms nor disproves McKinnon's claims about UFO-related material. The compromise itself is documented; the contents allegedly observed remain unverified. The distinction between confirmed network access and unverified discoveries is therefore central to understanding what the five compromised NASA centres actually reveal.

    });

      try { if (window.localStorage) localStorage.setItem('phoenix-ebay-market', marketId); } catch (err) {}
    }

      });

      marketId = available[0] || defaultMarket;
    }
    var select = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-select]');

        applyMarket(section, select.value, true);
      });

        button.addEventListener('click', function (event) {
          event.stopPropagation();
          applyMarket(section, button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-option'), true);
        });
      });
      document.addEventListener('click', function () { closeMenu(section); });
    }
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-button]')).forEach(function (button) {
      button.addEventListener('click', function () {
        applyMarket(section, button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button'), true);

    });
    applyMarket(section, marketId, false);
  });
