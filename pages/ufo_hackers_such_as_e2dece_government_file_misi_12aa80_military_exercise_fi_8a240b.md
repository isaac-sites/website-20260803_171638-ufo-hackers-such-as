## Introduction

Military exercise files are designed to look and feel like records from genuine operations. That realism is deliberate. Armed forces train commanders and staff by placing them in believable crises that combine authentic military organisations, real equipment, existing geography and plausible intelligence with events that never actually happened. The resulting documents—operations orders, intelligence summaries, casualty reports, logistics updates and situation reports—can appear indistinguishable from records of an actual conflict when viewed outside their original training context.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.army.mil/article/278363/training_fact_sheet_training_exercises" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: army.mil">[U.S. Army]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">army.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">U.S. ArmyTraining fact sheet: Training exercises &#124; Article &#124; The United States ArmyJuly 25, 2024...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: July 25, 2024</span></span></span>

This distinction matters when assessing claims linked to UFO-motivated hackers such as Gary McKinnon. Discovering an apparently dramatic military document on an official network does not automatically mean it records a real event. Defence organisations routinely create highly realistic fictional scenarios so personnel can practise decision-making under pressure. Detached from the exercise framework, those files can easily be mistaken for evidence of secret operations rather than training material.

## Why military exercises deliberately resemble real operations

Modern military exercises aim to prepare personnel for the uncertainty of combat rather than simply test procedures. To achieve this, planners create operational environments that are convincing enough for participants to make decisions as though they were responding to genuine events.

## How exercise scenarios and "injects" are constructed

Large military exercises rarely consist of one continuous fictional story. Instead, planners build a structured scenario supported by numerous "injects"—new pieces of information introduced during the exercise to force participants to react.

A typical exercise may generate documents such as:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* Intelligence reports describing fictional enemy movements.
* Damage assessments after simulated attacks.
* Requests for reinforcement or medical evacuation.
* Reports of infrastructure failures or cyber incidents.
* Diplomatic messages and media reporting.
* Casualty notifications and logistics shortages.

</div>

Many command-post exercises are driven by a Master Scenario Events List (MSEL), a carefully timed schedule that introduces these events in chronological order. Every inject is intended to create operational dilemmas requiring realistic military responses rather than simply testing whether participants know procedures.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.army.mil/article/278363/training_fact_sheet_training_exercises" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: army.mil">[U.S. Army]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">army.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">U.S. ArmyTraining fact sheet: Training exercises &#124; Article &#124; The United States ArmyJuly 25, 2024...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: July 25, 2024</span></span></span>

Because each inject is distributed using authentic staff processes and official document formats, individual records can appear entirely genuine when separated from the wider exercise.

## Which real details are mixed with fictional events

The realism of military training depends on blending authentic information with invented operational developments.

Common combinations include:

**Real organisations, fictional missions.** Existing brigades, squadrons or naval groups may participate under their genuine designations while responding to events that never occurred.

**Real geography, simulated conflict.** Exercises often use actual training areas or maps of real regions while imagining invasions, humanitarian crises or military escalations that are fictional.

**Real equipment, fictional losses.** Genuine aircraft, ships, vehicles and weapons systems may appear in documents reporting simulated damage or destruction.

**Real command procedures, fictional intelligence.** Staff officers practise analysing reports that resemble real intelligence products but describe imaginary enemy actions.

**Real communications, fictional casualties.** Medical evacuation requests and casualty reports are generated so commanders must prioritise resources under realistic pressure.

The result is documentation that accurately reflects how military headquarters communicate while recording events created solely for training purposes.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.army.mil/article/278363/training_fact_sheet_training_exercises" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: army.mil">[U.S. Army]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">army.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">U.S. ArmyTraining fact sheet: Training exercises &#124; Article &#124; The United States ArmyJuly 25, 2024...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: July 25, 2024</span></span></span>

## Why isolated exercise records can look like secret evidence

A single exercise document often lacks the surrounding context that identifies it as training.

Several factors contribute to misunderstanding:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--example" markdown="1">

* Classification markings may resemble those used during real operations.
* Official templates, distribution lists and military terminology are identical to operational paperwork.
* Individual pages may omit obvious references to the wider exercise.
* Copies circulated by email or extracted from archives can lose accompanying explanatory documents.
* Later readers may never see exercise instructions, planning orders or scenario guides that establish the fictional setting.

</div>

## NATO and US approaches to realistic training

Major military organisations openly describe their use of sophisticated fictional operational environments.

NATO's Joint Warfare Centre develops comprehensive synthetic scenarios that are explicitly described as fictitious while remaining highly realistic. These scenarios support collective defence, crisis response, cyber operations, hybrid warfare and multi-domain exercises across land, sea, air, space and cyberspace. Their purpose is to immerse headquarters in believable operational conditions rather than recreate specific historical conflicts.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.jwc.nato.int/our-work/scenario-development/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: jwc.nato.int">[NATO Joint Warfare Centre]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">jwc.nato.int</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Joint Warfare Centre Scenario Development: 360-Degree Multi-Domain Setting</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">NATO Joint Warfare CentreScenario Development: 360-Degree Multi-Domain Setting - NATO&#x27;s JWC...</span></span></span>

## What this means when evaluating unusual government files

Within discussions surrounding Gary McKinnon and similar cases, it is important to distinguish between a document's authenticity and the authenticity of the events it describes.

* Fictional intelligence sources.
