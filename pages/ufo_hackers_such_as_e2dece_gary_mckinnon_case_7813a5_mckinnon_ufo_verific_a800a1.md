## Introduction

Gary McKinnon's [unauthorised access]({{ 'unauthorised-access/' | relative_url }}) to US military and NASA computer systems is a documented historical fact. His most famous UFO-related claims, however, occupy a very different evidential category. He has consistently said that he briefly viewed an image of an unusual spacecraft, discovered an Excel spreadsheet titled "Non-Terrestrial Officers", and encountered records referring to ship-to-ship transfers. None of those materials has ever been independently examined, authenticated or reproduced. As a result, the public record separates a proven computer intrusion from extraordinary claims that rest almost entirely on McKinnon's own later descriptions rather than verifiable evidence.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.welivesecurity.com/2015/12/08/gary-mckinnon-reveals-detail-on-nasa-data-breach-and-extraterrestrial-life/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: welivesecurity.com">[WeLiveSecurity]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">welivesecurity.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Gary McKinnon reveals detail on NASA data breach and ‘extraterrestrial life’December 8, 2015...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: December 8, 2015</span></span></span>

Understanding this distinction is essential. The question is not whether McKinnon hacked government computers—he admitted doing so and the legal proceedings documented that conduct. The question is whether the alleged UFO-related material can be independently verified. On the evidence available today, it cannot.

## What evidence is documented

The documented part of the story is comparatively straightforward. US prosecutors alleged that McKinnon accessed dozens of military and NASA systems between 2001 and 2002, and McKinnon acknowledged entering those networks while searching for evidence of UFOs and advanced energy technology. His motivation has been discussed openly in numerous interviews over the years.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.welivesecurity.com/2015/12/08/gary-mckinnon-reveals-detail-on-nasa-data-breach-and-extraterrestrial-life/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: welivesecurity.com">[WeLiveSecurity]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">welivesecurity.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Gary McKinnon reveals detail on NASA data breach and ‘extraterrestrial life’December 8, 2015...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: December 8, 2015</span></span></span>

The UFO-related claims themselves have remained broadly consistent across interviews. McKinnon has described:

* seeing a high-resolution [NASA image]({{ 'nasa-image/' | relative_url }}) showing a smooth, cigar-shaped object;

## What evidence is missing

The central verification problem is that none of the key items allegedly discovered by McKinnon has ever entered the public evidential record.

### The alleged spacecraft image

McKinnon has said he viewed the image remotely over a slow dial-up connection while controlling a NASA computer. According to his account, he intended to capture the image but lost access when administrators disconnected the machine before the download completed. He therefore never preserved the full image or an authenticated screenshot.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.richardmedhurst.com/gary-mckinnon-interview/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: richardmedhurst.com">[Richard Medhurst]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">richardmedhurst.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Richard Medhurst EXCLUSIVE: GARY MCKINNON INTERVIEW</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Richard MedhurstEXCLUSIVE: GARY MCKINNON INTERVIEW - Richard Medhurst...</span></span></span>

Without the original file, investigators cannot determine:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* where the image originated;
* whether it had associated metadata;
* whether it depicted an unusual spacecraft, an ordinary satellite, image-processing artefacts, or another object entirely;
* whether it had been altered or mislabelled.

</div>

Because no preserved copy exists, none of these questions can be answered through forensic examination.

### The "Non-Terrestrial Officers" spreadsheet

The spreadsheet has become one of the most widely repeated elements of the story. McKinnon has described seeing names, ranks and references to transfers between ships, interpreting the title as suggesting a hidden space-based organisation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://projectcamelot.org/lang/en/gary_mckinnon_interview_transcript_en.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: projectcamelot.org">[Project Camelot]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">projectcamelot.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on projectcamelot.org.</span></span></span>

However, no authenticated copy of the spreadsheet has ever been released. No independent investigator has examined the file, confirmed its filename, reviewed its contents or established its provenance.

Even if such a spreadsheet once existed, multiple interpretations would remain possible. The title alone would not establish that it referred to extraterrestrials or a secret space fleet. It could, in principle, have been a project codename, an administrative label, a training file, fictional material or something else entirely. Because the original document is unavailable, those competing explanations cannot be tested.

### Alleged altered NASA photographs

McKinnon has also stated that he encountered both "processed" and "unprocessed" NASA images, believing that some photographs had been edited to remove unusual objects. Like the spacecraft image itself, no preserved examples of these allegedly altered [image pairs]({{ 'image-pairs/' | relative_url }}) have been produced for independent comparison.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.welivesecurity.com/2015/12/08/gary-mckinnon-reveals-detail-on-nasa-data-breach-and-extraterrestrial-life/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: welivesecurity.com">[WeLiveSecurity]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">welivesecurity.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Gary McKinnon reveals detail on NASA data breach and ‘extraterrestrial life’December 8, 2015...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: December 8, 2015</span></span></span>

Without both original files and their associated metadata, claims of photographic alteration remain impossible to evaluate using established digital forensic methods.

## Why independent verification is impossible

The absence of preserved evidence creates several practical barriers to verification.

First, there is no [chain of custody]({{ 'chain-of-custody/' | relative_url }}). Researchers cannot establish where the files came from, who created them or whether they were authentic government records.

Second, no independent witnesses have publicly confirmed seeing the same materials under comparable circumstances. The descriptions therefore rely primarily on McKinnon's testimony rather than multiple corroborating observers.

Third, there are no digital artefacts available for modern forensic analysis. Investigators cannot inspect file timestamps, cryptographic hashes, storage locations, image metadata or editing histories because the files themselves are unavailable.

Finally, the systems McKinnon accessed no longer exist in the same operational state. Even if similar files once existed, years of system upgrades, archival processes and network changes make retrospective confirmation exceptionally difficult.

These factors mean the claims cannot be disproved simply because evidence is absent—but neither can they be confirmed.

## How prior beliefs shape interpretation

McKinnon's account illustrates how existing beliefs influence interpretation of ambiguous information.

People already convinced that governments conceal evidence of UFOs often view the [missing files]({{ 'missing-files/' | relative_url }}) as consistent with secrecy. From that perspective, the inability to verify the evidence is itself interpreted as suspicious.

Sceptics generally reach the opposite conclusion. They argue that extraordinary claims require evidence that can be independently examined, and that unverifiable memories—however sincerely reported—cannot establish the existence of secret spacecraft or hidden space programmes.

There is also a middle position. Some observers accept that McKinnon probably believed he saw something unusual while recognising that memory, interpretation and context can all influence how ambiguous information is understood. A document title, an unfamiliar database or a partially viewed image may appear highly significant without necessarily meaning what an observer initially infers.

The crucial point is that prior beliefs affect interpretation after the evidence has disappeared. Because the underlying files are unavailable, the debate largely concerns competing interpretations of one person's recollections rather than competing analyses of shared evidence.

## The verification gap

The enduring appeal of McKinnon's story comes from the contrast between a proven cyber intrusion and unverified extraordinary discoveries. His access to government computers is well established, lending credibility to the claim that he was in a position to view genuine files. Yet the specific UFO-related materials that have attracted the most attention—the alleged spacecraft image, the "Non-Terrestrial Officers" spreadsheet and the purportedly altered photographs—were never preserved in a form that allows independent scrutiny.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.welivesecurity.com/2015/12/08/gary-mckinnon-reveals-detail-on-nasa-data-breach-and-extraterrestrial-life/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: welivesecurity.com">[WeLiveSecurity]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">welivesecurity.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Gary McKinnon reveals detail on NASA data breach and ‘extraterrestrial life’December 8, 2015...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: December 8, 2015</span></span></span>
