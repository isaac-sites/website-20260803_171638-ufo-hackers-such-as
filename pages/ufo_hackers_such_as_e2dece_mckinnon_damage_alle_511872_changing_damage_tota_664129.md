## Introduction

Early reporting on Gary McKinnon's hacking case frequently stated that the alleged damage totalled **about US$900,000**, while later court decisions and extradition documents usually referred to **more than US$700,000**. The change has sometimes been presented as evidence that the prosecution's case weakened dramatically. The available record supports a more limited conclusion: the headline figure evolved as investigators refined their assessment of recoverable losses and narrowed the figures relied upon during the extradition process. Neither amount was established through a criminal trial or a judicial damages award, because McKinnon was never tried in the United States. Instead, both figures remained prosecution estimates used at different stages of the case.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.justice.gov/archive/criminal/cybercrime/press-releases/2002/mckinnonIndict.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: justice.gov">[justice.gov]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">justice.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Department of JusticeLondon, England Hacker Indicted Under Computer Fraud and Abuse Act For Accessing Military Computers (November 12, 20...</span></span></span>

## Where the Two Headline Figures Appeared

The **approximately US$900,000** figure appeared in the US Department of Justice announcement issued after McKinnon's November 2002 indictment. That press release alleged that the combined losses suffered by military organisations, NASA and several private-sector victims amounted to roughly US$900,000. The estimate covered the broad investigation as it then stood and reflected losses attributed across multiple affected organisations.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.justice.gov/archive/criminal/cybercrime/press-releases/2002/mckinnonIndict.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: justice.gov">[Department of Justice]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">justice.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Department of JusticeLondon, England Hacker Indicted Under Computer Fraud and Abuse Act For Accessing Military Computers (November 12, 20...</span></span></span>

By contrast, later legal documents connected with the extradition proceedings—including the House of Lords judgment in 2008—consistently referred to the alleged **cost of repair as "over US$700,000."** Contemporary reporting of the extradition hearings likewise repeated the lower figure rather than the earlier press-release estimate.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://publications.parliament.uk/pa/ld200708/ldjudgmt/jd080730/mckinn-1.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: publications.parliament.uk">[parliament.uk]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">publications.parliament.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">UK Parliament House of Lords</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UK ParliamentHouse of Lords - Mckinnon V Government of The United States of America and Another...</span></span></span>

The coexistence of these figures reflects different stages of the same prosecution rather than two separate hacking incidents.

## How Prosecution Estimates Can Change

A reduction from approximately US$900,000 to more than US$700,000 is not unusual in a long-running cybercrime investigation. Several factors can explain such revisions.

First, investigators often begin with **preliminary loss estimates** gathered from affected agencies. As forensic work continues, duplicated costs, overlapping recovery work or speculative future expenses may be removed, leaving a narrower figure considered more defensible in court.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.justice.gov/archive/criminal/cybercrime/press-releases/2002/mckinnonIndict.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: justice.gov">[Department of Justice]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">justice.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Department of JusticeLondon, England Hacker Indicted Under Computer Fraud and Abuse Act For Accessing Military Computers (November 12, 20...</span></span></span>

Second, prosecutors may **refocus the calculation on losses directly tied to the charged conduct**. Computer crime statutes often permit losses to include incident response, system restoration, security reviews and operational disruption, but prosecutors may later exclude costs they consider harder to attribute with confidence. The extradition record therefore appears to rely on a more conservative aggregate figure than the original announcement.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://publications.parliament.uk/pa/ld200708/ldjudgmt/jd080730/mckinn-1.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: publications.parliament.uk">[UK Parliament]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">publications.parliament.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">UK Parliament House of Lords</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UK ParliamentHouse of Lords - Mckinnon V Government of The United States of America and Another...</span></span></span>

Third, the investigation covered numerous military and civilian systems. Individual indictments also identified specific damage calculations for particular incidents—for example, the New Jersey indictment alleged roughly **US$290,431** in losses relating to Naval Weapons Station Earle. Such component figures formed part of the overall case rather than replacing the total estimate, illustrating that the aggregate loss was assembled from multiple incidents whose valuation could change during the investigation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.justice.gov/archive/criminal/cybercrime/press-releases/2002/mckinnonIndict.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: justice.gov">[Department of Justice]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">justice.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Department of JusticeLondon, England Hacker Indicted Under Computer Fraud and Abuse Act For Accessing Military Computers (November 12, 20...</span></span></span>

## What the Discrepancy Does—and Does Not—Show

The differing totals are sometimes cited as proof that the government's allegations collapsed. The available documentary record does not support that interpretation.

The later extradition judgments continued to describe extensive alleged operational harm, including deleted operating-system files, deleted user accounts, impaired network availability and lengthy restoration work, while using the lower overall repair-cost figure of **more than US$700,000**. The prosecution therefore maintained that significant damage had occurred even after adopting the revised estimate.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://publications.parliament.uk/pa/ld200708/ldjudgmt/jd080730/mckinn-1.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: publications.parliament.uk">[UK Parliament]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">publications.parliament.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">UK Parliament House of Lords</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UK ParliamentHouse of Lords - Mckinnon V Government of The United States of America and Another...</span></span></span>

Equally, the reduction should not be interpreted as judicial confirmation that either figure was correct. British extradition courts were assessing whether the legal requirements for extradition were satisfied, not determining the precise financial loss suffered by US agencies. McKinnon admitted [unauthorised access]({{ 'unauthorised-access/' | relative_url }}) but denied intentionally causing the alleged damage, and because extradition was ultimately halted on [human-rights]({{ 'human-rights/' | relative_url }}) grounds, no US trial ever tested the competing accounts or established a final damages figure beyond reasonable doubt.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://publications.parliament.uk/pa/ld200708/ldjudgmt/jd080730/mckinn-1.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: publications.parliament.uk">[UK Parliament]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">publications.parliament.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">UK Parliament House of Lords</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UK ParliamentHouse of Lords - Mckinnon V Government of The United States of America and Another...</span></span></span>
