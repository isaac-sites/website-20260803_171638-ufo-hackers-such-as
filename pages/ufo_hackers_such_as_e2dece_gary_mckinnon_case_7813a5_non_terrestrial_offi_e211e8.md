## Introduction

Among all of Gary McKinnon's claims about what he encountered during his [unauthorised access]({{ 'unauthorised-access/' | relative_url }}) to US military and NASA computers, none has generated more speculation than an Excel spreadsheet allegedly titled **"Non-Terrestrial Officers"**. McKinnon has consistently described this file as containing a short list of names and ranks, alongside references elsewhere to "ship-to-ship" and "fleet-to-fleet" material transfers. Because neither the spreadsheet nor any screenshots have ever been publicly produced, the phrase has become the centre of competing interpretations rather than a verified piece of documentary evidence. The key question is therefore not whether the title proves a secret space programme, but what the wording could plausibly have meant if McKinnon's recollection was accurate.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.theguardian.com/theguardian/2005/jul/09/weekend7.weekend2" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: theguardian.com">[The Guardian]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">theguardian.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">The Guardian Game over &#124; Gary Mc Kinnon &#124; The Guardian</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The GuardianGame over &#124; Gary McKinnon &#124; The GuardianJuly 9, 2005...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: July 9, 2005</span></span></span>

## Who Were the Non-Terrestrial Officers?

### What McKinnon remembered reading

Across interviews over two decades, McKinnon's description has remained broadly consistent. He has said he found an Excel spreadsheet headed **"Non-Terrestrial Officers"** containing approximately 20 to 30 names and military ranks. He has also recalled another spreadsheet listing "ship-to-ship" and "fleet-to-fleet" transfers of materiel. According to McKinnon, he could not identify the listed ships in publicly available naval records, leading him to speculate that they belonged to an undisclosed organisation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://projectcamelot.org/lang/en/gary_mckinnon_interview_transcript_en.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: projectcamelot.org">[projectcamelot.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">projectcamelot.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Project Camelot &#124; Gary Mc Kinnon transcript</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Project Camelot &#124; Gary Mc Kinnon transcript</span></span></span>

An important point often overlooked is that McKinnon has repeatedly stated he did **not** interpret "non-terrestrial" to mean extraterrestrial beings. Instead, he argued that the phrase suggested personnel who were **not Earth-based**, perhaps stationed in orbit or assigned to a classified space operation. That distinction has been present in his own account since some of the earliest published interviews.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.theguardian.com/theguardian/2005/jul/09/weekend7.weekend2" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: theguardian.com">[The Guardian]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">theguardian.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">The Guardian Game over &#124; Gary Mc Kinnon &#124; The Guardian</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The GuardianGame over &#124; Gary McKinnon &#124; The GuardianJuly 9, 2005...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: July 9, 2005</span></span></span>

Because the alleged spreadsheet has never been independently examined, researchers cannot verify:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* whether the title was remembered precisely;
* whether the names corresponded to real personnel;
* which organisation owned the file;
* whether surrounding documents would have explained the terminology.

</div>

These missing pieces of context are central to evaluating the claim.

## Possible meanings of "non-terrestrial"

The phrase itself is unusual, but unusual wording is not, by itself, evidence of an extraordinary programme. Several interpretations have been proposed.

### Personnel assigned away from Earth

McKinnon's own interpretation was that "non-terrestrial" referred to officers serving somewhere beyond Earth's surface rather than aliens. He inferred that the spreadsheet documented personnel associated with an undisclosed military space capability. This became one of the foundations of later "secret space programme" narratives, although those broader claims extend well beyond what McKinnon actually said he observed.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.theguardian.com/theguardian/2005/jul/09/weekend7.weekend2" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: theguardian.com">[The Guardian]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">theguardian.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">The Guardian Game over &#124; Gary Mc Kinnon &#124; The Guardian</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The GuardianGame over &#124; Gary McKinnon &#124; The GuardianJuly 9, 2005...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: July 9, 2005</span></span></span>

### Administrative terminology

Another possibility is that the title reflected an internal administrative label rather than an operational description. Large organisations frequently create database headings that appear opaque outside their original context. Without neighbouring files, database documentation or explanatory notes, it is impossible to know whether "non-terrestrial" described personnel, locations, projects, simulated exercises or another classification entirely.

For example, organisations involved in satellite operations, orbital assets, launch support or space surveillance may distinguish activities occurring on Earth from those associated with spacecraft or orbital systems. While no publicly available documentation confirms that this exact phrase was ever used officially, administrative shorthand often differs from published terminology.

### Exercise or planning data

Military and aerospace organisations also generate planning documents for hypothetical scenarios, logistics exercises and future capabilities. A spreadsheet title alone cannot distinguish between:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--metric" markdown="1">

* an operational personnel roster;
* a planning exercise;
* a draft document;
* a fictional or training dataset;
* or a genuine administrative record.

</div>

Without the surrounding metadata, each possibility remains viable.

## Why metadata and context matter

The absence of the original spreadsheet is the principal obstacle to assessing McKinnon's claim.

Digital files normally contain contextual information that investigators rely upon, including:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* the complete filename rather than only a displayed title;
* creation and modification dates;
* author information;
* directory structure;
* associated documents;
* software version and storage location.

</div>

None of these details have been preserved publicly for the alleged spreadsheet. McKinnon has explained that his remote connection was slow and that he was interrupted before collecting extensive evidence, meaning later discussion has depended almost entirely on his recollection.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.welivesecurity.com/2015/12/08/gary-mckinnon-reveals-detail-on-nasa-data-breach-and-extraterrestrial-life/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: welivesecurity.com">[WeLiveSecurity]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">welivesecurity.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Gary McKinnon reveals detail on NASA data breach and ‘extraterrestrial life’December 8, 2015...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: December 8, 2015</span></span></span>

This creates an evidential asymmetry. The title is memorable precisely because it sounds extraordinary, but almost every piece of information needed to interpret it correctly is unavailable.

## Why the spreadsheet remains controversial

Supporters of McKinnon's account argue that the combination of an unusual personnel list and references to fleet transfers suggests documentation of a classified space organisation. They also point to the consistency of his retelling over many years.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.richardmedhurst.com/gary-mckinnon-interview/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: richardmedhurst.com">[Richard Medhurst]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">richardmedhurst.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Richard Medhurst EXCLUSIVE: GARY MCKINNON INTERVIEW</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Richard MedhurstEXCLUSIVE: GARY MCKINNON INTERVIEW - Richard Medhurst...</span></span></span>

## What can reasonably be concluded?

The "Non-Terrestrial Officers" spreadsheet occupies an unusual position in the McKinnon case because it is simultaneously one of his most distinctive claims and one of the least verifiable.

What can be said with confidence is relatively limited:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* McKinnon has consistently stated that he saw an Excel spreadsheet bearing that title.
* He interpreted "non-terrestrial" as meaning officers who were **not Earth-based**, not extraterrestrials.
* No publicly available copy of the spreadsheet has ever emerged.
* No official source has confirmed the terminology or McKinnon's interpretation.
* Without the original file and its metadata, several competing explanations remain plausible.

</div>

For that reason, the spreadsheet is best understood not as proof of a hidden space programme, but as an unresolved claim whose significance depends almost entirely on missing context. The extraordinary wording explains why it became central to later speculation, yet the absence of the underlying document prevents firm conclusions about what its title was actually intended to mean.
