## Introduction

The **$290,431** attributed to the alleged damage at **Naval Weapons Station Earle** was not presented by US prosecutors as the value of destroyed hardware or stolen information. Instead, it was an estimated financial loss associated with restoring affected computer systems, investigating the intrusion, recovering deleted data, and addressing the security consequences of the alleged compromise. ## What Made Up the $290,431 Earle Damage Claim?

The New Jersey indictment did not publish a detailed accounting spreadsheet showing how every dollar was calculated. Instead, it identified the categories of conduct that allegedly generated the loss. According to the indictment and accompanying Department of Justice announcement, the claimed damage arose from three principal elements:

* deletion of computer files needed for some systems to start correctly;
* deletion of computer logs recording the intrusions;
* security work required because remote-access software allegedly left the network vulnerable to further [unauthorised access]({{ 'unauthorised-access/' | relative_url }}).<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.justice.gov/archive/criminal/cybercrime/press-releases/2002/mckinnonIndict2.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: justice.gov">[Department of Justice]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">justice.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Department of JusticeBritish National Charged with Hacking Into N.J. This distinction matters because the prosecution was alleging operational disruption and recovery expense rather than physical destruction of computers.

### The deleted files and logs cited

The indictment alleged that on **23 September 2001**, McKinnon re-entered the Earle network using passwords obtained during earlier intrusions and previously installed remote-access software. Prosecutors said that during this access he deleted files required for certain computers to power up successfully. They also alleged that he erased computer logs documenting his presence on the network.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.justice.gov/archive/criminal/cybercrime/press-releases/2002/mckinnonIndict2.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: justice.gov">[Department of Justice]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">justice.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Department of JusticeBritish National Charged with Hacking Into N.J. Those allegations appeared alongside broader claims that approximately **950 passwords** had earlier been copied from server computers at Earle after the installation of the commercial remote administration program **RemotelyAnywhere**. According to prosecutors, the combination of deleted startup files, missing audit logs and compromised credentials significantly complicated recovery efforts.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.justice.gov/archive/criminal/cybercrime/press-releases/2002/mckinnonIndict2.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: justice.gov">[Department of Justice]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">justice.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Department of JusticeBritish National Charged with Hacking Into N.J. The court was careful to summarise the allegations rather than determine that they had been proved at trial.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://publications.parliament.uk/pa/ld200708/ldjudgmt/jd080730/mckinn-1.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: publications.parliament.uk">[UK Parliament]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">publications.parliament.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">UK Parliament House of Lords</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UK ParliamentHouse of Lords - Mckinnon V Government of The United States of America and Another...</span></span></span>

## Recovery and Security Costs Included

The most significant feature of the Earle figure is that it represented the estimated cost of responding to the incident rather than a simple valuation of deleted files.

The Department of Justice alleged that the network of roughly **300 computers** was effectively shut down for about a week following the September 2001 intrusion. It further claimed that, for several additional weeks, personnel were restricted largely to internal email before external connectivity and automated naval message routing were fully restored. If accepted, such disruption would naturally require extensive work by system administrators, investigators and security personnel.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.justice.gov/archive/criminal/cybercrime/press-releases/2002/mckinnonIndict2.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: justice.gov">[Department of Justice]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">justice.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Department of JusticeBritish National Charged with Hacking Into N.J. Naval Weapons Station Computers, Disabling Network After Sept. 11 (N...</span></span></span>

The indictment also alleged that RemotelyAnywhere remained installed on systems after the intrusion, creating an ongoing avenue through which McKinnon—or another intruder possessing the credentials—could potentially regain access. Consequently, the claimed loss was not limited to repairing deleted files. It also encompassed work required to identify compromised systems, remove unauthorised software, restore trust in authentication systems, investigate the extent of the intrusion and strengthen network security.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.justice.gov/archive/criminal/cybercrime/press-releases/2002/mckinnonIndict2.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: justice.gov">[Department of Justice]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">justice.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Department of JusticeBritish National Charged with Hacking Into N.J. Under the CFAA, this approach was legally significant because recoverable "loss" can include the reasonable costs of responding to an offence, conducting damage assessments and restoring systems to their prior condition, even where no physical equipment has been destroyed. ## Why an Indictment Figure Is Not a Court Award

Although the **$290,431** figure is frequently repeated in accounts of the McKinnon case, it should be understood for what it was: a prosecution estimate contained in an indictment.

The New Jersey case never proceeded to a completed criminal trial because McKinnon's extradition from the United Kingdom was ultimately blocked in 2012. As a result, no jury evaluated the evidence supporting each component of the claimed loss, no witnesses were cross-examined on the calculation methodology and no court entered a damages award confirming that the government had actually incurred exactly **$290,431** in recoverable costs.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.gov.uk/government/news/latest-on-gary-mckinnon-case" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: GOV.UK">[GOV.UK]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">GOV.UK</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Latest on Gary Mc Kinnon case</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Latest on Gary McKinnon case - GOV.UKNovember 4, 2010...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: November 4, 2010</span></span></span>

    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-domain-label]')).forEach(function (label) {
      label.textContent = domainLabel(marketId);
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-option]')).forEach(function (button) {
      button.setAttribute('aria-selected', button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-option') === marketId ? 'true' : 'false');
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-button]')).forEach(function (button) {
      var active = button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button') === marketId;
      button.setAttribute('aria-pressed', active ? 'true' : 'false');
    });
    section.setAttribute('data-ebay-selected-market', marketId);
    if (persist) {
      try { if (window.localStorage) localStorage.setItem('phoenix-ebay-market', marketId); } catch (err) {}
    }
    closeMenu(section);
  }
  sections.forEach(function (section) {
    if (section.getAttribute('data-ebay-localized-init') === '1') return;
    section.setAttribute('data-ebay-localized-init', '1');
    var available = availableMarkets(section);
    var marketId = defaultMarket;
    if (window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation && window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation.inferMarket) {
      marketId = window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation.inferMarket(available, {
        storageKey: 'phoenix-ebay-market',
        defaultMarket: defaultMarket
      });
    } else if (available.indexOf(defaultMarket)< 0) {
      marketId = available[0] || defaultMarket;
    }
    var select = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-select]');
    if (select) {
      select.addEventListener('change', function () {
        applyMarket(section, select.value, true);
      });
    }
    var trigger = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-trigger]');
    var menu = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-menu]');
    if (trigger && menu) {
      trigger.addEventListener('click', function (event) {
        event.stopPropagation();
        var expanded = trigger.getAttribute('aria-expanded') === 'true';
        menu.hidden = expanded;
        trigger.setAttribute('aria-expanded', expanded ? 'false' : 'true');
        var picker = trigger.closest('.fr-ebay-market-picker');
        if (picker) picker.classList.toggle('fr-ebay-market-picker--open', !expanded);
      });
      Array.prototype.slice.call(menu.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-option]')).forEach(function (button) {
        button.addEventListener('click', function (event) {
          event.stopPropagation();
          applyMarket(section, button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-option'), true);
        });
      });
      document.addEventListener('click', function () { closeMenu(section); });
    }
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-button]')).forEach(function (button) {
      button.addEventListener('click', function () {
        applyMarket(section, button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button'), true);
      });
    });
    applyMarket(section, marketId, false);
  });
})();
