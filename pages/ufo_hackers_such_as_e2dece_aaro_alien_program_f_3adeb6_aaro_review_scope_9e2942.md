## Introduction

Congress did not ask the All-domain Anomaly Resolution Office (AARO) simply to assess whether unidentified anomalous phenomena (UAP) had been reported. It directed the office to investigate a much narrower and more consequential claim: whether the United States government had secretly recovered extraterrestrial craft or biological material, hidden those programmes from Congress, and attempted to reverse-engineer alien technology. To answer that question, AARO examined historical records stretching back to 1945, interviewed witnesses, reviewed classified and unclassified archives, and worked with officials responsible for overseeing the government's most sensitive [classified programmes]({{ 'secret-projects/' | relative_url }}). Its review found no verifiable evidence that such hidden alien-recovery or reverse-engineering programmes existed, while also identifying several cases where genuine classified defence projects had been misunderstood or later retold as UFO-related programmes.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://uap-archive.org/uap/records/aaro-historical-record-report-vol1/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: uap-archive.org">[UAP Records Archive]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">uap-archive.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UAP Records ArchiveAARO Report on the Historical Record of U.S. Government Involvement with UAP, Volume I &#124; UAP Records ArchiveFebruary 1...</span></span></span>

Rather than treating these as abstract rumours, AARO attempted to identify the named people, facilities, documents, contractors and programmes repeatedly cited by witnesses. According to the office, investigators tracked down the organisations, programme records and officials associated with those claims wherever possible.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://uap-archive.org/uap/records/aaro-historical-record-report-vol1/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: uap-archive.org">[UAP Records Archive]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">uap-archive.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UAP Records ArchiveAARO Report on the Historical Record of U.S. Government Involvement with UAP, Volume I &#124; UAP Records ArchiveFebruary 1...</span></span></span>

Importantly, the review distinguished between unexplained aerial reports and allegations of institutional concealment. A sighting that remains unidentified is not, by itself, evidence of a crash-retrieval programme. AARO's remit in this part of the historical review was to determine whether documentary and institutional evidence supported claims of secret alien technology programmes.

## Archives, interviews and oversight records examined

The review relied on several independent sources of evidence rather than witness testimony alone.

### Government archives

AARO reported reviewing classified and unclassified government archives covering official UAP investigations from 1945 onwards. These included historical Air Force investigations such as Projects SIGN, GRUDGE and BLUE BOOK, later studies including the Condon review, and more recent Defence Department and Intelligence Community efforts such as AAWSAP, AATIP and the UAP Task Force.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://uap-archive.org/uap/records/aaro-historical-record-report-vol1/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: uap-archive.org">[UAP Records Archive]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">uap-archive.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UAP Records ArchiveAARO Report on the Historical Record of U.S. Government Involvement with UAP, Volume I &#124; UAP Records ArchiveFebruary 1...</span></span></span>

The purpose was not merely to catalogue past investigations but to determine whether any official programme had documented verified extraterrestrial technology or concealed evidence of recovered craft.

### Witness interviews

Investigators conducted approximately 30 interviews with individuals claiming knowledge of hidden programmes. AARO also continued pursuing additional leads beyond the publication of Volume I.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Page%3AAARO_Historical_Record_Report_Volume_1_2024.pdf/6" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: en.wikisource.org">[Wikisource]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">en.wikisource.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Page:AARO Historical Record Report Volume</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Page:AARO Historical Record Report Volume 1 2024.pdf/6 - Wikisource, the free online library...</span></span></span>

The office emphasised an important limitation: according to its findings, none of the interviewees possessed first-hand authorised access to the alleged reverse-engineering programmes they described. Many accounts were based on conversations, second-hand information or long-circulating stories rather than direct operational involvement.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Page%3AAARO_Historical_Record_Report_Volume_1_2024.pdf/9" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: en.wikisource.org">[Wikisource]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">en.wikisource.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Page:AARO Historical Record Report Volume</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Page:AARO Historical Record Report Volume 1 2024.pdf/9 - Wikisource, the free online library...</span></span></span>

### Classified programme oversight

One distinctive aspect of the review was AARO's cooperation with officials responsible for Controlled Access Programmes and Special Access Programmes across the Department of Defense and Intelligence Community.

Rather than relying solely on programme names supplied by witnesses, investigators worked with the officials responsible for administering highly [classified projects]({{ 'misidentified-projects/' | relative_url }}) to determine whether the alleged programmes actually existed, whether their missions matched the claims, or whether witnesses had confused unrelated national security activities with purported extraterrestrial research. AARO stated that it received unrestricted access necessary for these investigations.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Page%3AAARO_Historical_Record_Report_Volume_1_2024.pdf/9" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: en.wikisource.org">[Wikisource]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">en.wikisource.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Page:AARO Historical Record Report Volume</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Page:AARO Historical Record Report Volume 1 2024.pdf/9 - Wikisource, the free online library...</span></span></span>

### Industry enquiries

Because many allegations involved defence contractors, AARO also contacted companies identified by interviewees.

According to the report, appropriately cleared executives, chief technology officers, senior scientists and engineers from the named organisations were interviewed. The office reported that these companies denied possessing or reverse-engineering extraterrestrial technology, and investigators said they found no supporting contractual or programme documentation indicating otherwise.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Page%3AAARO_Historical_Record_Report_Volume_1_2024.pdf/34" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: en.wikisource.org">[Wikisource]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">en.wikisource.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Page:AARO Historical Record Report Volume</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Page:AARO Historical Record Report Volume 1 2024.pdf/34 - Wikisource, the free online libraryMay 6, 2024...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: May 6, 2024</span></span></span>

## How AARO tested specific secret-programme allegations

Rather than dismissing claims outright, the review attempted to trace each allegation to verifiable evidence.

Where witnesses identified programme names, AARO searched for the underlying records. Where facilities or laboratories were named, investigators examined whether documentary evidence linked those locations to alleged crash-retrieval work. Claims involving specific technological tests or recovered materials were similarly examined against available records and physical evidence.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://uap-archive.org/uap/records/aaro-historical-record-report-vol1/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: uap-archive.org">[UAP Records Archive]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">uap-archive.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UAP Records ArchiveAARO Report on the Historical Record of U.S. Government Involvement with UAP, Volume I &#124; UAP Records ArchiveFebruary 1...</span></span></span>

One example involved an alleged fragment of extraterrestrial spacecraft obtained from a private UAP organisation. AARO subjected the material to technical analysis and concluded that it was an ordinary manufactured terrestrial alloy composed primarily of magnesium, zinc and bismuth rather than evidence of off-world technology.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://uap-archive.org/uap/records/aaro-historical-record-report-vol1/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: uap-archive.org">[UAP Records Archive]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">uap-archive.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UAP Records ArchiveAARO Report on the Historical Record of U.S. Government Involvement with UAP, Volume I &#124; UAP Records ArchiveFebruary 1...</span></span></span>

The office also investigated allegations involving named former military personnel. ## Why some alleged programmes appeared genuine

One of AARO's more nuanced conclusions was that several stories originated from misunderstandings of authentic classified programmes.

The office concluded that alleged hidden reverse-engineering programmes generally fell into three categories:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* programmes that never existed;
* genuine highly classified national security programmes unrelated to extraterrestrial technology;
* proposals or initiatives that were never approved or were later terminated.

</div>

This distinction matters because individuals without authorised access could observe fragments of highly compartmented activities without understanding their actual purpose, creating fertile ground for speculation. According to AARO, discussion of such programmes outside secure environments also increased the risk of misunderstanding sensitive defence work.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Page%3AAARO_Historical_Record_Report_Volume_1_2024.pdf/9" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: en.wikisource.org">[Wikisource]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">en.wikisource.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Page:AARO Historical Record Report Volume</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Page:AARO Historical Record Report Volume 1 2024.pdf/9 - Wikisource, the free online library...</span></span></span>

The report highlighted the proposed **[KONA BLUE]({{ 'kona-blue/' | relative_url }})** programme as an example. Witnesses described it as evidence of a hidden crash-retrieval effort, but AARO concluded it was instead a proposed Department of Homeland Security programme advocated by individuals who believed the government already possessed alien technology. The proposal was ultimately rejected because supporters failed to provide empirical evidence supporting its underlying claims.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Page%3AAARO_Historical_Record_Report_Volume_1_2024.pdf/34" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: en.wikisource.org">[Wikisource]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">en.wikisource.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Page:AARO Historical Record Report Volume</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Page:AARO Historical Record Report Volume 1 2024.pdf/34 - Wikisource, the free online libraryMay 6, 2024...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: May 6, 2024</span></span></span>

## What the review could and could not establish

AARO's conclusions were narrower than either believers or sceptics sometimes suggest.

The review concluded that investigators found:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* no empirical evidence that the US government or private industry had reverse-engineered extraterrestrial technology;
* no evidence supporting claims that companies possessed recovered off-world hardware;
* no verified documentation establishing hidden crash-retrieval programmes;
* no evidence that alleged programmes had been improperly concealed from authorised government oversight.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://uap-archive.org/uap/records/aaro-historical-record-report-vol1/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: uap-archive.org">[UAP Records Archive]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">uap-archive.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UAP Records ArchiveAARO Report on the Historical Record of U.S. Government Involvement with UAP, Volume I &#124; UAP Records ArchiveFebruary 1...</span></span></span>

</div>

At the same time, the report did **not** claim that every UAP report has been explained. It acknowledged that some incidents remain unresolved because available sensor data or eyewitness information is insufficient for firm identification. Nor did Volume I claim to investigate every individual allegation ever made; it assessed the evidence available through the historical review and indicated that additional claims would be addressed separately.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Page%3AAARO_Historical_Record_Report_Volume_1_2024.pdf/6" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: en.wikisource.org">[Wikisource]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">en.wikisource.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Page:AARO Historical Record Report Volume</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Page:AARO Historical Record Report Volume 1 2024.pdf/6 - Wikisource, the free online library...</span></span></span>

For readers interested in cases such as those cited by UFO-motivated hackers including Gary McKinnon, the key implication is that AARO specifically examined the institutional claims underlying alleged secret alien programmes—not merely reports of unusual objects. Its historical review found that the documentary record, classified programme oversight and available physical evidence did not substantiate claims of a longstanding government effort to recover and reverse-engineer extraterrestrial technology.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://uap-archive.org/uap/records/aaro-historical-record-report-vol1/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: uap-archive.org">[UAP Records Archive]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">uap-archive.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UAP Records ArchiveAARO Report on the Historical Record of U.S. Government Involvement with UAP, Volume I &#124; UAP Records ArchiveFebruary 1...</span></span></span>
